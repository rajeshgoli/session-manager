2479 — Near-idle queue jobs give back their slot

Author: Claude Opus 5.5 92ff4310 · Ticket #2479 · 21 Oct 2026

Memo

Recommendation: any running queue job that has used under half a CPU core, little GPU and under 5 GiB of memory for 30 minutes stops counting against the queue's job limits, so waiting work can start. When it gets busy again (roughly double those numbers) it counts again. sm never stops or slows it. Over the last 23 days this would have unblocked a waiting job in 32 of the 56 hours that some job sat waiting for a slot. 9 of the 115 jobs that went near-idle later got busy again, three of them heavily (see the risk section).

The same change fixes #2480, which has the queue stuck right now: once start-now put a fourth service job on a service limit of 2, every admission pass refuses to start any job. After the fix, services over their limit block only further service starts.

Slot
One unit of the queue's job limits. Two limits apply to every job: the global limit (max_running_jobs, 8 on this Mac) and its type's limit (background 2, service 2, tests 6, perf 1). A running job takes one slot from each. A pending job starts only when both have a free slot. A running perf job also pauses every new start while it runs (its "quiet window").
Near-idle job
A running job of any type that, over the last 30 minutes, averaged under 50% of one CPU core and under 10% of the GPU, and never held 5 GiB of memory or more. It holds no slot. Log output does not matter: a watcher that prints a status line every minute is still near-idle.
Wake
A near-idle job wakes when, over any 5 minutes, it averages above one full core or above 20% of the GPU, or when it holds more than 10 GiB. It takes its slots back at once, even if that puts the queue over its limits. Nothing already running is stopped: the queue simply starts nothing new until it is back under.
Quiet (existing, unchanged)
The existing "Quiet since …" alert: no CPU, GPU or log output for 10 minutes. It warns that a job may be hung. Near-idle is a separate, capacity-only judgment; a job can be near-idle and not quiet.

Risk: a near-idle job wakes up big

9 of the 115 jobs that went near-idle in the last 23 days later passed one core or 10 GiB. Six were small: five rose to 1–2 cores and one grew to 19 GiB while staying under a core. Three were real compute: 2171-watch (31 cores, 34 GiB), 2221-smoke-watch (29 cores, 13 GiB) and today's 2171-watch8 (20 cores, 38 GiB). These "watchers" sit quiet for over 30 minutes, then do heavy local work. Under the rule each takes its slot back when it wakes, but whatever started during its quiet spell keeps running, so for a while the Mac carries both. Memory stays protected: no job starts while free memory is under the reserve, and a job that declared --memory is stopped when it exceeds it. CPU does not: those spells can oversubscribe the 32 cores and slow everything running. I recommend accepting this. The alternative, holding slots for jobs that might wake, is today’s behaviour, and it cost up to 32 hours of waiting.

FAQ — what changed since the last revision

Thresholds are looser. Near-idle now means under half a core, 10% GPU and 5 GiB (was 5%, no GPU, 2 GiB). Waking needs double (was 25%, any GPU, 4 GiB).

Every job type is eligible, including perf, whose quiet window lifts while it is near-idle (Decision 4). Tests and perf were exempt before.

#2480 is fixed in this PR rather than in a separate one first (Decision 7).

Does waking evict anything? No. The concern was a waking job pushing out work already started. Nothing is ever stopped; the queue only holds back new starts until it is under its limits again.

Appendices

A. The rule, exactly

Inputs. The utilization recorder already writes one job_samples row per running job every 5 seconds: cumulative CPU seconds, cumulative GPU seconds, footprint bytes (RSS when footprint is missing)1. Near-idle is computed from those rows only. Log bytes are not an input.

Config (new block under queue_runner; all optional, defaults shown):

queue_runner:
  near_idle:
    enabled: true
    minutes: 30            # idle window
    cpu_percent: 50        # % of ONE core, averaged over the window
    gpu_percent: 10        # % of the GPU, averaged over the window
    memory_gib: 5          # footprint must stay below this at every sample
    wake_minutes: 5
    wake_cpu_percent: 100  # % of one core, averaged over any wake window
    wake_gpu_percent: 20   # % of the GPU, averaged over any wake window
    wake_memory_gib: 10    # footprint at any single sample

enabled: false restores today's behaviour exactly. Validation: cpu_percent < wake_cpu_percent, gpu_percent < wake_gpu_percent, memory_gib < wake_memory_gib, minutes ≥ wake_minutes ≥ 1; otherwise config load fails with a message naming the key.

Entering near-idle. A running job of any type except shell-adopted jobs becomes near-idle at the first sample t where all hold:

  1. t − started_at ≥ minutes, and the recorder has samples of this job covering [t − minutes, t] with no gap over 60 s (the same coverage rule the existing low-CPU check uses2). A gap restarts the window.
  2. CPU growth over the window, summing only positive deltas between consecutive samples (a process exit drops the cumulative counter), is < cpu_percent/100 × window seconds.
  3. GPU growth over the window, summed the same way, is < gpu_percent/100 × window seconds. (GPU seconds per wall second is the share of the GPU the job used.)
  4. Every sample's footprint in the window is < memory_gib. A missing reading (both footprint and RSS null) counts as a failure, not a pass.

Waking. A near-idle job stops being near-idle at the first sample t where any holds: positive CPU growth over [t − wake_minutes, t] > wake_cpu_percent/100 × that span; positive GPU growth over the same span > wake_gpu_percent/100 × that span; footprint at t > wake_memory_gib. Until the job has wake_minutes of samples since it went near-idle, the span is whatever it has. After waking it must satisfy the entry rule again from scratch (a fresh full window starting after the wake).

Worked example. A watcher starts at 10:00 and spends 2 minutes at 3 cores and 6 GiB setting up, then polls a cloud API at 2% of a core and 400 MiB. At 10:32 the 30-minute window [10:02, 10:32] holds only polling: CPU 2% < 50%, no GPU, 400 MiB < 5 GiB. It becomes near-idle at 10:32 and an admission pass runs. At 11:40 it parses a result for one minute at 2 cores: the 5-minute average is 0.4 cores, below one core, so it stays near-idle. At 13:10 it downloads results and its footprint reaches 12 GiB: it wakes at that sample and counts again. If the queue now holds 9 slot holders against a limit of 8, nothing is stopped and nothing new starts until one finishes or something else goes near-idle. Earliest it can be near-idle again: 13:40.

Not eligible: shell-adopted jobs (they already take no slot3) and pending jobs.

Restart. The near-idle flag is held in memory by the recorder and is not persisted. On a server start every running job holds a slot. Because the window reads stored job_samples rows, a job whose last 30 minutes of samples straddle the restart can be near-idle at the first recorder tick, provided the coverage rule holds (a restart gap over 60 s breaks coverage, so in practice the window restarts). Either outcome is correct; no test pins which.

B. Admission changes

One predicate. Define "holds a slot" as: state == running, not shell-adopted, and not near-idle. The global limit additionally ignores review jobs, as today. Every admission check that reads running jobs reads slot holders instead; the only checks that still see every running job are the memory guard and the per-job limits, because those measure real use.

E. How the evidence was computed

Read-only copy of ~/.local/share/claude-sessions/utilization.db, snapshot 21 Oct evening, 554 hours of samples, all four sampled job types (review jobs are not sampled). For every running job, slide a 30-minute window over its samples and apply rule A with 50% of a core and 5 GiB; the simulation used "no GPU" because no job in the window used any. "Passed 1 core or 10 GiB" in the memo table means a later 5-minute window at or above 1 core, or a footprint at or above 10 GiB. "Hours some job waited" sums sample intervals (capped at 10 s) where any pending job had holding_reason = concurrency_cap; that reason covers per-type limits as well as the global one, so 32 of 56 is an upper bound on hours actually unblocked. The nine wake-ups: 2066-nq5-money (19 GiB), 2221-smoke-watch (29 cores), 2171-watch (31 cores, 34 GiB), 2171-watch8 (20 cores, 38 GiB), backup-first-run (1.3 cores), and four tests jobs at 1.3–2 cores.

Z. FAQ

Why not just raise the global limit to 16? It treats a busy 48 GiB job and a sleeping watcher alike; two more busy jobs would oversubscribe the Mac with no signal. The limit means "real work running at once"; near-idle keeps it meaning that.

Why does memory count at all if CPU is the contended resource? A job holding 9 GiB while idle (2171-watch8 today) is often between phases of real work; 2171-watch8 itself later ran 20 cores. Counting it is conservative, and the 5 GiB bar still frees every pure watcher seen today.

Why not displace near-idle background jobs for perf? Displacement exists to give perf a quiet machine. A near-idle job already leaves it quiet, and stopping a watcher can lose a cloud run's bookkeeping. If it wakes during the perf run, the perf numbers may be noisy; the submitter of a perf job reads its result in that light.

Why not persist the flag across restarts? It is derived entirely from stored samples, so there is nothing to persist that the samples do not already hold; recomputing from them is simpler and cannot go stale.
