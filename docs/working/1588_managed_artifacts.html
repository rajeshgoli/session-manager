1588 — sm-managed artifact storage

Author: sm-1588-spec-author 541e4b49 · Ticket #1588 · 26 Sep 2026 · Revision 1

Memo

Session Manager takes ownership of ~/Artifacts and keeps the disk from filling. Every folder in it gets a record. The record says what the folder is, who made it, how to rebuild it, and what keeps it alive. sm deletes folders once nothing keeps them alive. When the disk runs short, sm deletes folders that are cheap to rebuild. Anything expensive comes to you as one list with a Delete button. New builds wait in sm queue until their output fits. The free-text RETENTION.md notes give way to a record that sm checks every day.

Most of today's disk is cheap to rebuild, which is what makes this work. The NQ input chain from #1692 takes 290 GB and took 21 minutes to build, going by its own command log.1 Under this design, sm can delete it when space is short and an agent can rebuild it with one command. You would not be asked.

Why now

Retention notes cover about 89% of the bytes, so the notes themselves are not the gap. The gap is that nothing can act on them. A note says "keep while an NQ/YM pretraining ticket reads them; delete by 2027-03-31 at the latest". A person can read that. No program can decide from it today whether the folder can go. Meanwhile, nothing stops a 300 GB build from starting when the disk has 200 GB free.

Terms used in this memo
Managed root
A directory sm owns, such as ~/Artifacts. It is set in sm's config together with its GitHub repo. fractal-algo-rust is the first repo with one.
Artifact
One top-level folder in the managed root. It is the unit sm tracks, sizes and deletes. Its name is the folder name, for example 1692_nq_bundle.
Record
sm's database row for an artifact: owner session, ticket, epic label, size, a free-text description written by the agent, its holds, its review date, and its recipe.
Hold
One reason to keep an artifact that sm can check by itself. An artifact stays while at least one hold is active. The kinds are listed in the next section.
Review date
A date on every record. If the artifact is still being kept on that date, sm asks you whether to keep it. sm never deletes because a review date arrived.
Recipe
How the artifact was built: the sm queue run jobs that wrote it, the git commit each ran at, the artifacts each read (its inputs), and each job's measured run time. sm records the recipe itself, so the agent writes none of it.
Cheap to rebuild
The recipe is complete, its commit is preserved on GitHub, and its recorded build time is at most 4 hours of Mac time with no GPU spend.
Floor
The free space sm protects: 5% of the disk, about 180 GB.
Pressure pass
What sm runs when free space falls below the floor, or when a new build would push it below: delete what is expired, then what is cheap to rebuild, then ask you.
Digest
At most one message a day from sm to you in the sm app. It lists what sm deleted and what needs you. It sends nothing on a quiet day.

How an artifact lives

Only two ways into the root. An agent either (1) runs sm queue run --artifact <name> --est-size <size> …, or (2) runs sm artifact new <name> for hand-made work, such as files pulled back from a Runpod pod. In both cases, when the work is finished, the agent runs sm artifact done with a description and at least one hold. A folder that arrives any other way is unregistered. It appears in your digest and is deleted seven days after sm first sees it unless an agent runs sm artifact adopt on it.

The holds. An agent declares three kinds. sm adds three more by itself.

HoldActive whileExample
until:2027-03-31the date has not passedA fixture kept until year end
open:#1665that ticket or PR is open on GitHub"keep until epic 1665 merges"
projectalways; only you or an agent's explicit delete removes itThe ES bundle, the resident detector
named on main (automatic)a file on main names the artifact's path, not counting docs/working and docs/execution1299_model_bundle, named by docs/specs/1302…
input of (automatic)a kept or evicted artifact lists it as an input1691_scratch, read to build 1665-labels
in use (automatic)a waiting or running queue job reads or writes itAny artifact a running training job reads

A condition sm cannot check, such as "until Rajesh has reviewed the result", goes in the description. The agent pairs it with a hold sm can check (usually open: on the ticket) and a review date. When the review date comes, the artifact shows up in your digest with the note and a Delete / Keep 30 more days choice.

Under disk pressure, sm runs three steps in order. It stops as soon as free space is back above the floor, plus whatever a waiting build needs:

StepDeletesNever touches
1Expired artifacts, without waiting out their 24-hour notice.Artifacts in use by a queue job; project holds; hand-made or adopted artifacts (no recipe); anything whose inputs are missing; an input of anything kept.
2Artifacts that are cheap to rebuild, most gigabytes per build-hour first. Their state becomes evicted.
3Nothing. It sends you one message: how much space is needed, and the largest artifacts sm may not delete itself, each with the reason and a Delete button.

The queue hold. A job that declares --artifact starts only if, after its estimated output and what running jobs still expect to write, free space stays above the floor. Otherwise sm runs the pressure pass. If the job still does not fit, it waits with the reason disk_floor. Such a job waits up to 24 hours instead of the queue's usual 5-minute admission window. It starts on its own as soon as space frees, for example when you press Delete. For a rebuild, sm uses the last measured size, so no estimate is needed.

When the job is submitted, sm checks the space: free space minus 300 GB minus what running jobs still expect to write. The job starts only if the result is above 180 GB. When the job finishes, sm records the commit it ran at and the 21 minutes it took. When the agent runs done … --recipe-complete, the agent is attesting that re-running this job at this commit reproduces the folders. sm then pushes a git tag so the commit survives the squash merge and branch deletion. Two months later, suppose the disk is short. sm deletes all three folders together (about 14 GB per build-minute) and marks them evicted. When #1705's next run needs them, its agent runs sm artifact rebuild 1692_nq_bundle, which queues the same job at the same commit.

3. A build that must wait

Suppose the disk has 300 GB free and a running job still expects to write 60 GB. A new 150 GB build arrives. 300 − 60 − 150 = 90 GB, which is below the 180 GB floor, so sm needs 90 GB more. Step 1 finds nothing expired. Step 2 evicts the cheap-to-rebuild YM chain (about 311 GB), and the build starts. If nothing cheap were left, the build would wait, and you would get one message:

Artifacts: build "1710-es-states" (150 GB) is waiting for disk. Needs 90 GB.
  1691_scratch     301 GB  input of 1665-labels · review 2027-03-31    [Delete]
  1299_model_…      75 GB  named in docs/specs/1302_shared_frame_inputs.html [Delete]
  1629-completion   14 GB  made by hand, no recipe · open #1629          [Delete]
Already decided (in session, 26 Sep 2026)
  1. Artifacts are sm-managed. Only sm writes into the root, through sm queue run --artifact or sm artifact new. The daily scan flags anything else and deletes it after a grace period.
  2. sm may delete a still-wanted artifact that is cheap to rebuild when the disk is tight. Everything else goes to you.
  3. A rebuild recipe is the queue job, its commit, and the artifacts it read. sm records it from the queue, and the agent writes none of it.
  4. The queue holds a build until its output fits above a 5% floor.
  5. No secondary drive. This is a general sm feature, and fractal-algo-rust is the first user.
  6. Runpod storage volumes are out of scope. You manage them.
  7. No per-epic disk budgets for now. Each record carries an epic label, and the list shows totals by epic.

Risks

  • An agent reading a folder outside a queue job gets no protection. For example, it reads the folder interactively, without --input. sm can evict the folder under that agent. The agent then fails, reads the record, and rebuilds. That costs only minutes, because only cheap artifacts are evicted.
  • A wrong "recipe complete" claim makes an eviction permanent. For example, the job needed a binary built outside the queue. The mitigation is the dry-run week, plus a rule in the agent instructions that the recipe's jobs must build what they use. A failed rebuild is reported to you in the digest.
  • A hold that reads GitHub fails safe. If GitHub cannot be reached, sm treats every ticket as still open and deletes nothing on that basis.

A. States and transitions

An artifact is identified by (root, name), where name is a single path segment matching ^[A-Za-z0-9][A-Za-z0-9_.-]{0,127}$. Names compare case-insensitively, because APFS is case-insensitive and agents write both ~/Artifacts and ~/artifacts. The stored name keeps the case the creator used.

FromToTriggerEffects
(none)buildingsm artifact new, or submission of a queue job naming an absent artifact in --artifactCreates the folder (mode 0755), inserts the record with owner_session_id = requester and ticket resolved per D.1, and logs created.
(none)unregisteredThe scan finds a top-level entry with no record and no alias rowInserts a record with first_seen_at = now and grace_until = first_seen + unregistered_grace_days (or the initial grace, B).
buildingkeptsm artifact done succeeds (D.3)Sets description, holds, review date and finalized_at. If --recipe-complete is given, validates the recipe and pushes tags (E.6).
unregisteredkeptsm artifact adoptSame as done, but the artifact has no recipe and is never rebuildable.
unregistereddeletedgrace_until passes (and the artifact is not in use)Deletion (G). Reason unregistered.
buildingdeletedAn agent's sm artifact delete; or the orphan rule: the owner session has ended, no job references it, and 7 days have passed since the session endedDeletion (G).

expiredkeptAny hold becomes active again, or an agent adds a hold, or you press Keep 30 more daysClears expired_at and delete_after.

Review due is a flag, not a state: state = kept and review_by <= today. Such an artifact appears under "Needs you" in every digest until you act or its holds lapse. Deleted is terminal for a name. A new artifact may reuse the name only with sm artifact new --replace-deleted, which archives the old row's history under a suffixed name (<name>@<deleted_at>).

Sets. Two artifacts belong to the same set when any queue job wrote to both. Sets are the connected components of the job-to-artifact graph. A set is finalized when all its members are finalized. It is rebuildable only when every member is rebuildable. It is evicted, rebuilt and tagged as a unit. A hand-made or adopted artifact is a set of one.

  orphan_building_days: 7
  max_review_days: 183
  disk_hold_max_wait_hours: 24
  overrun_factor: 1.5
  pressure_check_seconds: 600
  scan_time: "04:17"             # local time; the daily scan (I) and hold evaluation (F)
  digest_time: "08:07"           # local time
  roots:
    - path: ~/Artifacts
      repo: rajeshgoli/fractal-algo-rust
      repo_checkout: ~/projects/fractal-algo-rust   # used only for rebuild worktrees (J)
      default_branch: main
      named_exclude: ["docs/working/", "docs/execution/"]
      floor_percent: 5
  • Path resolution. db_path, mirror_dir, roots[].path and repo_checkout go through expand_home. The first three also go through isolate_path_from_protected_root, like app_artifacts.root_dir at config.rs:341-347. That way scripts/test-rust-isolated.sh never touches the live root.
  • Validation at startup (refuse to start on failure and print the reason): each root exists and is a directory; no two roots are on the same filesystem (compare st_dev); floor_percent is in 1–50; the times parse as HH:MM; deletion is one of the two values.
  • Dry run means every deletion sm would start on its own (expiry, unregistered, orphan, pressure) is logged as would_delete, reported in the digest, and not performed. The artifact's state does not change. Agent deletions (sm artifact delete) and your Delete button run even in dry run, because each is an explicit act. The disk_floor queue hold runs in dry run, because it deletes nothing. Decision 6 is met by shipping ticket 3 with deletion: dry_run. The maintainer switches it to enabled seven days later, and the digest states the switch.
  • sm --check-config reports the section, each root's filesystem size, the free space and the floor in bytes.

C. Persisted shapes

This uses its own database file, artifacts.db, following owner_push.db and mobile_devices.db. The schema is created with CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS, and later columns are added with ensure_column (queue.rs:6287). Times are RFC 3339 UTC strings, as in queue_jobs.

CREATE TABLE artifacts (
  root              TEXT NOT NULL,          -- canonical root path
  name              TEXT NOT NULL COLLATE NOCASE,
  state             TEXT NOT NULL,          -- unregistered|building|kept|expired|evicted|deleted
  set_id            TEXT NOT NULL,          -- see A (Sets); equals name for a set of one
  origin            TEXT NOT NULL,          -- queue|hand|adopted|unregistered
  owner_session_id  TEXT,
  owner_session_name TEXT,
  repo              TEXT NOT NULL,          -- owner/name
  ticket            INTEGER,                -- issue or PR number in repo
  epic              TEXT,                   -- free label
  description       TEXT,                   -- markdown, <= 8000 chars
  review_by         TEXT,                   -- YYYY-MM-DD; NULL only for project-held
  recipe_complete   INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 0,
  gpu_hours         REAL,                   -- hand-entered, informational
  gpu_cost_usd      REAL,                   -- hand-entered; > 0 makes the set not cheap
  size_bytes        INTEGER,
  size_measured_at  TEXT,
  est_size_bytes    INTEGER,
  created_at        TEXT NOT NULL,
  first_seen_at     TEXT,                   -- unregistered only
  grace_until       TEXT,                   -- unregistered only
  finalized_at      TEXT,
  expired_at        TEXT,
  delete_after      TEXT,
  evicted_at        TEXT,
  deleted_at        TEXT,
  deleted_by        TEXT,                   -- sm|session id|owner
  delete_reason     TEXT,                   -- expired|pressure|unregistered|orphan|agent|owner
  PRIMARY KEY (root, name)
);
CREATE TABLE artifact_holds (             -- declared holds only
  root TEXT NOT NULL, name TEXT NOT NULL COLLATE NOCASE,
  kind TEXT NOT NULL,                     -- until|open|project
  value TEXT NOT NULL,                    -- YYYY-MM-DD | owner/repo#N | ''
  added_by TEXT NOT NULL, added_at TEXT NOT NULL,
  PRIMARY KEY (root, name, kind, value)
);
CREATE TABLE artifact_auto_holds (        -- recomputed by F; replaced wholesale per artifact
  root TEXT NOT NULL, name TEXT NOT NULL COLLATE NOCASE,
  kind TEXT NOT NULL,                     -- named|input_of|in_use
  evidence TEXT NOT NULL,                 -- file path on main | dependent name | queue job id
  evaluated_at TEXT NOT NULL
);
CREATE TABLE artifact_jobs (              -- the recipe; one row per (artifact, job)
  root TEXT NOT NULL, name TEXT NOT NULL COLLATE NOCASE,
  job_id TEXT NOT NULL,                   -- queue_jobs.id
  seq INTEGER NOT NULL,                   -- submission order within the set
  type TEXT NOT NULL, label TEXT NOT NULL,
  argv_json TEXT, script_text TEXT,       -- script file contents captured at submission
  env_json TEXT NOT NULL, timeout_seconds INTEGER NOT NULL,
  cwd TEXT NOT NULL, cwd_rel TEXT,        -- cwd relative to the git top level
  commit_sha TEXT, dirty INTEGER,         -- at submission; NULL sha when cwd is not in git
  tag TEXT,                               -- pushed tag, E.6
  state TEXT NOT NULL,                    -- mirrors queue_jobs.state at last update
  exit_code INTEGER, wall_seconds INTEGER,
  bytes_written INTEGER,                  -- measured at finish (E.5)
  PRIMARY KEY (root, name, job_id)
);
CREATE TABLE artifact_inputs (
  root TEXT NOT NULL, name TEXT NOT NULL COLLATE NOCASE,
  input TEXT NOT NULL COLLATE NOCASE,
  source TEXT NOT NULL,                   -- declared|detected
  job_id TEXT NOT NULL,
  PRIMARY KEY (root, name, input, job_id)
);
CREATE TABLE artifact_aliases (           -- top-level symlinks resolving into an artifact
  root TEXT NOT NULL, alias TEXT NOT NULL COLLATE NOCASE,
  target_name TEXT NOT NULL COLLATE NOCASE, target_rel TEXT NOT NULL,
  PRIMARY KEY (root, alias)
);
CREATE TABLE artifact_events (
  id INTEGER PRIMARY KEY AUTOINCREMENT,
  root TEXT NOT NULL, name TEXT NOT NULL, at TEXT NOT NULL,
  kind TEXT NOT NULL,     -- created|finalized|hold_added|hold_removed|expired|revived|
                          -- evicted|deleted|would_delete|rebuild_queued|rebuilt|
                          -- rebuild_failed|owner_extend|overrun|tag_pushed|tag_removed
  actor TEXT NOT NULL,    -- sm | session id | owner
  detail_json TEXT NOT NULL
);
CREATE TABLE artifact_job_reservations (  -- queue jobs that declared outputs
  job_id TEXT PRIMARY KEY, root TEXT NOT NULL,
  outputs_json TEXT NOT NULL, inputs_json TEXT NOT NULL,
  est_size_bytes INTEGER NOT NULL,
  start_bytes INTEGER,                    -- sum of output sizes when the job started
  first_disk_hold_at TEXT
);

Derived values (not stored, computed on read):

  • rebuild_seconds(set) = the sum of wall_seconds over the distinct succeeded jobs that wrote any member.
  • cheap(set) = all members have recipe_complete = 1, and rebuild_seconds ≤ cheap_rebuild_hours × 3600, and no member has gpu_cost_usd > 0 or gpu_hours > 0.
  • active_holds(a) = the declared holds that are active (F) plus the rows in artifact_auto_holds.

D. The sm artifact commands

All commands call HTTP endpoints on the local server (K.2) and accept --root <path>. The root resolves as follows: --root if given; else the root whose repo equals the GitHub repo of the current directory (gh_repo_view_in_dir, http.rs:1344); else the only configured root; else an error listing the roots. A hold value open:#N means open:<root repo>#N, and open:owner/repo#N is accepted. Every command exits 0 on success. On refusal it exits 2 and prints a single line that starts with error: and gives the reason.

D.1 Ticket and epic resolution

ticket is --ticket N if given. Otherwise it is the requester's single active claim in the root's repo (WorkClaimStore::claims_for_session, work_claims.rs:708). With zero or several claims it is NULL, and done then requires --ticket. epic is --epic if given, else NULL. The list groups NULL-epic artifacts under #<ticket>, or under "no ticket".

D.2 sm artifact new <name> [--ticket N] [--epic L] [--describe TEXT|@FILE]

Creates a hand-made artifact in state building and prints its absolute path. It refuses if the name is invalid, if a record exists in any state other than deleted, or if a filesystem entry with that name exists. Use adopt for an existing folder.

D.3 sm artifact done <name>... --describe TEXT|@FILE --hold SPEC... [--review-by YYYY-MM-DD] [--recipe-complete] [--ticket N] [--epic L] [--gpu-hours H --gpu-cost USD]

  • Each named artifact must be building. No waiting or running queue job may name it as an output. The description must be 1–8000 characters.
  • At least one --hold is required. The SPEC grammar is until:YYYY-MM-DD (a date after today), open:[owner/repo]#N, or project.
  • --review-by defaults to the latest until: date. It is required when there is no until: and no project. It must be after today and within max_review_days, and so must any until: date. A project hold makes review_by NULL.
  • --recipe-complete applies to every named artifact. It requires the named artifacts to be exactly one whole set: all members named, each with at least one succeeded job, and every succeeded job with a non-NULL commit_sha and dirty = 0. Then E.6 runs. If any check or tag push fails, the whole command fails and changes nothing.
  • On success: finalized_at = now, state = kept, and hold evaluation (F) runs for these artifacts at once.

Example error: error: 1692_nq_bundle's set also contains 1692_nq_inputs, 1692_nq_context; name all of them with --recipe-complete

D.4 sm artifact adopt <name> ...same flags as done except --recipe-complete

This applies to an unregistered artifact. It becomes kept with origin = adopted and the adopting session as owner. If the entry is a symlink that resolves into a registered artifact, adopt refuses and names the target. Aliases are recorded automatically (I.3).

D.5 sm artifact hold <name> add|remove SPEC, and sm artifact review <name> --review-by DATE

These add or remove declared holds on a kept, expired or evicted artifact. Adding to an expired artifact revives it (A). Removing the last declared hold is allowed; F decides the result. project may be removed by any agent (logged). Changing the review date follows the limits in D.3.

D.6 sm artifact delete <name> --reason TEXT

This deletes now (G), in any state except deleted. It refuses when:

  • the artifact is in use by a waiting or running job (the error names the job);
  • it has a project hold (answer: remove the hold first, which is visible in your digest);
  • it is an input of a kept or evicted artifact (the error names the dependents).

Deleting an evicted artifact only marks it deleted. The reason is stored and shown in the digest.

D.7 sm artifact list [--state S] [--epic L] [--json], sm artifact show <name> [--json], sm artifact disk [--json]

list prints one line per non-deleted artifact: name, state, size, epic, ticket, holds summary, review date, and rebuild cost ("21 min", "not rebuildable", or "hand-made"). show prints the whole record, holds with evidence, the recipe (jobs, commits, tags, inputs) and the last 20 events. disk prints for each root: filesystem size, free space, floor, the reservations of running jobs, and the jobs waiting on disk_floor. --json emits the same objects as the HTTP API (K.2).

D.8 sm artifact rebuild <name>

See J.

E. Queue integration

E.1 New sm queue run flags

These are added to QueueRunArgs (bin/sm.rs:550-603) and sent in the POST /queue-jobs body (bin/sm.rs:1652):

  • --artifact NAME (repeatable). An output artifact. It must be absent (the job creates it, state building), or already building. It is refused if it is kept, expired or evicted ("finalized; choose a new name"). The exception is a rebuild, which J issues itself.
  • --input NAME (repeatable). An input artifact. It must be kept, or building with no job writing it. It is refused if evicted ("run sm artifact rebuild NAME first") or deleted.
  • --est-size SIZE. Accepts K, M, G and T suffixes (powers of 1024). Required when --artifact is given, except for jobs issued by J, which use the recorded size.

A job with no --artifact behaves exactly as today. --input without --artifact is allowed. It marks the input as in use for the job's lifetime, which is how a training job protects what it reads.

E.2 At submission (create_queue_job, http.rs:6920)

  1. Resolve the root (D rules, using the job's cwd).
  2. Detect inputs. Search argv and the script file's text, case-insensitively, for the root's path in three spellings: absolute, ~/… and $HOME/…. Each spelling is followed by /NAME, where the next character is not in [A-Za-z0-9_.-]. Every NAME that is a registered artifact or an alias (resolved to its target) and is not an output is added to the inputs with source = detected. A detected input that is evicted or deleted does not refuse the job. It is recorded, and the job's requester receives the one-line warning [sm artifact] job <label> references evicted artifact <name>.
  3. Capture git state in cwd: git rev-parse --show-toplevel, git rev-parse HEAD, and git status --porcelain --untracked-files=no (non-empty output means dirty = 1). If cwd is not in a git repo, commit_sha is NULL. Put a shared pub(crate) fn git_in(dir, args) helper beside git_command_success (sessions.rs:11644).
  4. Capture the script file's contents (up to 1 MiB; a larger file refuses the job with a message).
  5. In one transaction: create the absent output artifacts; insert the artifact_job_reservations row; insert artifact_jobs rows (state queued) for every output; insert artifact_inputs rows; and recompute set_id for the affected artifacts (the union of the existing sets and this job's outputs). The queue job row is created in the same request. If the artifact write fails, the queue job is not created.

E.3 The admission check (admit_pending_queue_jobs_conn, queue.rs:3243)

This check applies only to jobs with a reservation row. It runs after the existing checks pass, meaning the job would otherwise start now. With all values in bytes on the root's filesystem:

free        = statvfs(root).f_bavail × f_frsize          (nix::sys::statvfs, "fs" feature already enabled)
floor       = floor_percent / 100 × f_blocks × f_frsize
outstanding = Σ over running jobs r with reservations on this filesystem:
                max(0, r.est_size − (current_output_size(r) − r.start_bytes))
start iff   free − outstanding − job.est_size ≥ floor

current_output_size uses the size walk (I.2) on the job's outputs, cached per job for 5 minutes. If the check fails, the job gets the hold reason disk_floor, first_disk_hold_at is set if it is empty, and the pressure pass (H) runs with needed = floor − (free − outstanding − job.est_size), at most once per 10 minutes per job. Admission is retried on the queue's usual cadence. When the job starts, start_bytes is set to the current sum of its outputs' sizes.

The wait window. A job whose current hold reason is disk_floor does not expire at max_wait_seconds. It expires disk_hold_max_wait_hours after first_disk_hold_at, with state wait_expired, and its wake names disk_floor. If its hold reason changes to another reason, the normal window applies again, measured from queued_at. The existing expiry path is expire_pending_queue_jobs_conn (queue.rs:3433), and disk_floor joins the holding reasons listed by sm queue status. The requester gets the existing "queued" notification with reason disk_floor once.

Perf interaction. The check never makes a perf job wait for disk ahead of the existing perf rules. The existing order of checks is unchanged; disk_floor is evaluated last.

E.4 While running

Outputs and inputs of waiting and running jobs carry the automatic in use hold (F). If a size walk finds current_output_size − start_bytes > overrun_factor × est_size, sm sends one message to the requester ([sm artifact] <label> has written X, estimated Y), logs an overrun event, and does nothing else.

E.5 At completion (finish_queue_job_conn_with_policy, queue.rs:5143)

This updates the job's artifact_jobs rows with state, exit code and wall_seconds (finished_at − started_at). It measures the outputs' sizes, updates size_bytes, and stores bytes_written = new size − start_bytes. The reservation row is deleted. A failed, timed-out or cancelled job stays in artifact_jobs for history but does not count toward the recipe or rebuild_seconds. The recording happens in the same code path as the completion wake, so a crash between them is repaired by the existing retry loop (retry_unnotified_queue_job_completions_conn).

E.6 Commit tags

On done --recipe-complete, for each distinct commit_sha among the set's succeeded jobs, the tag name is artifact/<set_id>/<sha7>. If git ls-remote --tags origin <tag> already shows it, sm records it and skips the push. Otherwise sm pushes from the job's recorded cwd if that directory still exists, else from the mirror (F.3) if the mirror has the commit, using git push origin <sha>:refs/tags/<tag>. If neither can push, the command fails with error: commit <sha7> is not available to push; rebuild from a pushed commit. When a set becomes deleted (not evicted), sm deletes its tags with git push origin :refs/tags/<tag> from the mirror. A failed tag removal is logged and retried at the next daily scan. It never blocks anything.

F. Hold evaluation

F.1 When

Holds are evaluated at scan_time daily for every non-deleted artifact. They are also evaluated at the start of every pressure pass if the last evaluation is older than one hour, and immediately for the artifacts touched by done, adopt, hold and delete. The in use hold is always computed live from the queue tables. It is never cached.

F.3 The named on main check

sm keeps a bare mirror per repo at <mirror_dir>/<owner>__<name>.git. It is created with git clone --bare --filter=blob:none on first use and updated with git fetch origin '+refs/heads/<default_branch>:refs/heads/<default_branch>' before each daily evaluation. This never touches repo_checkout. One pass:

git -C <mirror> grep -I -i -o -E 'artifacts/[A-Za-z0-9][A-Za-z0-9_.-]*' <default_branch> -- . ':!docs/working/' ':!docs/execution/'

The exclusions come from named_exclude. The output lines are <branch>:<path>:<match>. The final path segment of each match is taken as a name. A name matching an artifact or alias adds a named row with evidence = <path>, one row per (artifact, file) and at most 20 per artifact. The match relies on the literal segment artifacts/, which fits a root whose final segment is Artifacts. For any other root, the pattern uses that root's final path segment. Example: docs/specs/1302_shared_frame_inputs.html contains ~/artifacts/1299_model_bundle_0f71094b/bin, which produces the row (1299_model_bundle_0f71094b, named, docs/specs/1302_shared_frame_inputs.html).

F.4 Result

After evaluation, a kept artifact with no active hold becomes expired. An expired artifact with an active hold becomes kept (event revived). An evicted artifact with no active hold becomes deleted. A building or unregistered artifact is not affected by holds. For each change, the owner session (if alive) gets a one-line [sm artifact] message. It is sent as a system message through the same delivery path as queue wakes.

G. Deletion mechanics and safety

  • Target. The target is always <root>/<name>, where name is a single validated segment. sm resolves the root with canonicalize once at startup and refuses any target whose parent is not exactly that path.
  • Removal. sm walks the tree without following symlinks and removes files, links and directories. A symlink inside the artifact that points elsewhere is removed as a link, and its target is untouched. It then removes every alias symlink recorded in artifact_aliases for the artifact. Deletion is permanent. Nothing goes to the Trash, because the Trash is on the same disk.
  • Re-check at the moment of deletion. Immediately before removing anything, sm re-checks in one database transaction that the artifact is still in the state that justified deletion and is not in use. This closes the race with a job submitted a second earlier.
  • Errors. On a partial failure (for example, permission denied), the artifact keeps its state. sm logs the error as an event, and the digest lists it under "Needs you". There are no retries within the day.
  • Dry run: B.
  • History. Every deletion writes one event carrying size_bytes, the reason and the actor, and the digest reports it.

H. The pressure pass

Triggers: every pressure_check_seconds, when free < floor on a root's filesystem, with needed = floor − free; and from E.3, with its needed. Only one pass runs per filesystem at a time. A trigger that arrives during a pass waits for it and then re-measures.

  1. Refresh holds if they are stale (F.1). Recompute free.
  2. Step 1. Delete every expired artifact that is not in use, largest first, re-measuring free after each. Stop when free − outstanding ≥ floor + needed_by_waiting_jobs, where needed_by_waiting_jobs is the largest est_size among jobs waiting on disk_floor, or 0.
  3. Step 2. Candidates are sets where: every member is kept; cheap(set) holds; no member has a project hold or is in use; no member is an input of an artifact outside the set that is building, kept or expired; and every input of every member is kept, building or expired, meaning present on disk. Order the candidates by size_bytes(set) / max(rebuild_seconds(set), 60), descending, with ties broken by name. Evict whole sets in that order, re-measuring after each, with the same stop rule.
  4. Step 3. If the stop rule is still unmet, send you one message (K.1 format "disk") listing up to 10 non-deleted, non-building artifacts on that filesystem, largest first. Each line has the reason sm would not delete it: project, not rebuildable (hand-made / adopted / recipe incomplete), over 4 h (N h), GPU cost, input of X, or in use by job L. At most one such message goes out per filesystem per 6 hours, and a new one replaces the pending one in the list view.

In dry run, steps 1 and 2 log would_delete and assume the space was freed when choosing what comes next. That way the digest shows the full list sm would have deleted.

Worked ordering (sizes from 26 Sep; build times as recorded, or assumed where marked): YM chain 311 GB / 21 min (assumed equal to NQ) → 14.8 GB per minute; NQ chain 292 GB / 21 min → 13.9; 1680-forecast-fixture is hand-made, so it is not a candidate. The pass evicts YM first, then NQ.

I. The daily scan

I.1 Schedule and perf courtesy

The scan runs at scan_time on a dedicated OS thread (the thread-and-sleep idiom of main.rs:157-196). If a perf job is running or waiting for its quiet window, the scan waits until it finishes. A walk of about 1 TB competes for I/O, and perf jobs need a quiet machine. If the scan has not run for 36 hours, it runs regardless, and the digest says so.

I.2 Size walk

Size is the sum of st_blocks × 512 over every entry, walking with lstat and never following symlinks. Each (st_dev, st_ino) is counted once per artifact. Other mounts are not crossed (an entry whose st_dev differs from the root's is skipped). A hand-rolled read_dir recursion is enough; no new crate is needed.

I.3 Classification of top-level entries

After sizing, the scan runs hold evaluation (F), expiry deletions that are due, unregistered deletions whose grace has passed, and orphan deletions (A), in that order. Everything the scan does feeds the next digest.

J. Rebuild

sm artifact rebuild <name> accepts an evicted artifact, and rebuilds its whole set. It refuses with a reason if any member is not evicted, if the set is not recipe_complete, or if any input is evicted or deleted. In that last case the error names them, and the caller rebuilds those first.

  1. Create a detached worktree at ~/worktrees/sm-rebuild-<set_id>-<sha7> with git -C <repo_checkout> worktree add --detach <path> <sha>, fetching the tag first. With several commits, create one worktree per commit.
  2. Set every member to building. Then queue the set's succeeded jobs in their original seq order. Each job keeps its original type, timeout, env and argv, and gets its script text written to <worktree>/.sm-rebuild/<seq>.sh. cwd = <worktree>/<cwd_rel>. The job carries the same --artifact outputs, with est_size set to the recorded bytes_written. Its notify target is the requesting session. Each job is queued when the previous one succeeds. The chain is driven by the completion path (E.5), not by a watcher.
  3. When the last job succeeds, return every member to kept with its previous holds, description and review date. Log rebuilt, remove the worktree, and send the requester [sm artifact] rebuilt <set> in N min.
  4. When any job fails, stop the chain and leave the members building with origin unchanged. Log rebuild_failed, keep the worktree for diagnosis, notify the requester, and list the failure in the digest under "Needs you". The failed set is no longer cheap until a later rebuild succeeds (a rebuild_failed event newer than the last rebuilt excludes it from step 2).

Z. FAQ

Why top-level folders only? Retention notes today sometimes govern a subfolder (1691_scratch/ref) or specific runs (1303_block1). Tracking arbitrary subpaths would make deletion, sizing and aliasing depend on nesting rules that two engineers would implement differently. One folder, one lifetime is a rule agents can follow. Material with a different life goes in its own top-level folder.

Why not follow the commands.jsonl that fractal agents already write? It is a fractal convention, not an sm primitive, and it records steps that may not run through the queue. The queue is the one place sm sees start, finish, commit and exit code for certain.

Why a bare mirror instead of grepping ~/projects/fractal-algo-rust? That checkout is Rajesh's, and its branch and working state are not main. The mirror is read-only to everything else and always at origin/main.

Why do evicted artifacts keep holding their inputs? An evicted artifact is still wanted. Its rebuild needs its inputs present. If the inputs could expire while it sits evicted, "cheap to rebuild" would silently become "cannot rebuild".

Can this cope with a job that writes outside its declared outputs? Not in real time. sm does not sandbox jobs. A new top-level folder shows up at the next scan as unregistered. Growth inside another kept artifact shows up as a size change in sm artifact show, and it is not acted on.

What about files agents keep inside worktrees? They are out of scope. sm worktree keep already governs worktrees, and cargo clean covers build output.
