1588 — sm-managed artifacts: records, freezing, placement and backup

Author: kubla-khan 7d859b3c · Ticket #1588 · 15 Oct 2026 · Revision 2

Memo

Session Manager keeps every artifact folder in the cheapest form its use allows, and asks you only when no agent can decide. When a folder appears in ~/artifacts, sm asks the agent that made it for a record: what it is, which tickets will read it, and what a rebuild would cost. When nothing will read a folder any more, sm freezes it: it packs the folder into compressed archives on the cold store, an always-connected 4 TB external SSD. A data-pipeline folder holding 128 GB of data freezes to 7 GB in under a minute, and comes back in about a minute when a job needs it. When a disk runs short anyway, sm deletes in order of gigabytes freed per minute you would wait to rebuild, so a 20-minute pipeline output goes long before a 9-hour training output. Jobs declare the disk they will write, the way they declare memory. sm tells you when the backup drive is worth connecting, then backs up overnight only what cannot be rebuilt.

The point is space without losing anything. The eight largest folders take 1.6 TB on disk today. Frozen, they would take about 0.3 TB (Figure 1). Nothing is thrown away to get there, and the one folder type that barely compresses, float32 .npy feature summaries, shows up as exactly that.

Why now

The Mac's 3.6 TB disk reached 97% full, with 125 GB free. 722 GB of artifact folders and an 823 GB data directory moved to a new external SSD by hand, with symlinks left behind so every path still works. That bought 2 TB, but deciding what to move meant reading 280 folders' free-text retention notes, which no program can act on. A note says "keep until the next experiment's readout"; a person can read that, sm cannot. The same work showed that the safe moves are mechanical: copy, verify every file, swap in a symlink. That is work for sm, not for the owner or an agent's afternoon.

Terms used in this memo
Artifact root
~/artifacts: the folder agents read and write. Set in sm's config with its GitHub repo; the owner's research repo is the first user.
Cold store
A second disk that stays connected, here a 4 TB external SSD. sm moves folders there and keeps frozen archives there. A moved folder keeps its ~/artifacts path through a symlink.
Artifact
One top-level folder in the root, wherever it physically lives. Its name is the folder name, for example 4210_dataset.
Record
sm's entry for an artifact: who made it, its ticket, a description, its consumers, how it was built, GPU use, size, and where it lives.
Consumer
A ticket that will read the artifact, for example #4210. Agents name them; sm checks on GitHub whether they are still open.
Recipe
How the artifact was built: the sm queue run jobs that wrote it, the git commit each ran at, the artifacts each read, and each job's measured run time. sm records it from the queue; the agent writes none of it.
Live, frozen
Live: an ordinary folder, readable now. Frozen: compressed archives on the cold store; the folder at its path holds only a note saying how to thaw it.
Thaw
Unpacking a frozen artifact back to live, onto the cold store. About a minute per 100 GB of data. sm does it by itself when a queue job needs the artifact.
Hot, cold
Cold: every consumer ticket is closed, no queue job has used the folder for 14 days, and nothing else keeps it (a "keep for the project" mark, a keep-until date, or a spec on main naming it). Anything else is hot.
Score
Gigabytes an action frees, divided by the minutes you would wait if the artifact is needed again: thaw minutes for a freeze, rebuild minutes for a deletion. Higher goes first.
Enough
The free space sm restores on each disk when it falls short: room for the largest output any single job wrote in the last 30 days. About 350 GB today.
Floor
The free space no job may cross when it starts: 5% of the disk, about 180 GB on the Mac.
Digest
At most one message a day from sm to you in the sm app, and only when something needs you or happened that you should know.

How an artifact lives

1. Every folder gets a record when it appears

sm looks at the top level of ~/artifacts every minute. A new folder with no record is unrecorded. sm works out who made it, in this order: the queue job that declared it as its output; else the agent that holds the ticket whose number starts the folder name (nearly every folder follows NNNN_name today); else nobody, and it goes on your list. It then messages that agent:

[sm artifact] ~/artifacts/4210_dataset appeared (47 GB) with no record. Record it:
  sm artifact record 4210_dataset --describe @README.md --consumer #4211 [--gpu-hours H --gpu-cost USD]

Hot or cold, score, backup duesm, daily—

A folder still unrecorded after 7 days is frozen and listed for you once, with Delete and Thaw buttons. Freezing instead of deleting means a forgotten record costs nothing permanent. The record also serves agents: one that finds a folder later reads what it is and who needs it, and can freeze, thaw, rebuild or delete it without asking you.

2. Cold folders are frozen, not deleted

Every night sm freezes each live folder that is cold. Freezing writes the folder as zstd archives on the cold store, checks every file in the archives against the folder, and only then removes the folder, leaving a one-page FROZEN.md at its path that says how to thaw it. A queue job that names a frozen folder, in its --input or anywhere in its command, waits while sm thaws it, then starts. An agent working by hand runs sm artifact thaw <name>. Thawed folders land on the cold store, behind a symlink, so they never fill the Mac, and they count as used, so they do not freeze again for 14 days.

On the first user's disk today almost nothing is cold by this rule: one burst of experiment jobs read every large folder within the last week. The 104 cold folders hold under 1 GB. Freezing pays off as tickets close. For example, once the next experiment names its consumers, an older 308 GB pipeline folder that nothing open reads any more goes cold, and shrinks to about 33 GB.

3. Under disk pressure: gigabytes per minute of waiting

When a disk falls below enough, or a job needs more room than there is, sm lists every action it may take and does the highest score first, re-measuring after each, until there is room. There is no fixed "cheap to rebuild" cutoff: light pressure only takes the obvious actions, heavy pressure goes further down the same list.

The table shows why freezing does most of the work: freezing a chain frees about 90% of it for a few minutes of thawing, and deleting the frozen copy afterwards adds little. If the list runs out and there is still not enough, you get one message: how much is needed, and the largest folders sm may not touch, each with the reason and a Delete button.

4. Jobs declare disk, like memory

A job that writes an artifact says how much: sm queue run --artifact 4210_dataset --disk 60G …. Space other running jobs have declared but not yet written counts as taken. The job starts when its declared size fits above the floor. If it does not fit, sm runs the pressure list. If the Mac is still short but the cold store has room, sm places the job's output folder on the cold store behind a symlink before it starts, so the job writes there without knowing. Otherwise the job waits, up to 24 hours, and starts by itself when space frees. A job that writes more than 1.5 times what it declared gets one warning; sm does not stop it, because running out of disk does not take the machine down the way running out of memory does.

5. Backups: sm says when, you plug in, it runs overnight

sm backs up what cannot be rebuilt: ~/data, ~/projects, ~/.config, and every artifact with no recipe or with GPU spend, live or frozen. Artifacts with a recipe are skipped: their inputs and their commit are already safe, so a lost one costs a rebuild, not a loss. sm tracks what changed since the last backup. When something to back up has waited 7 days, the digest says so:

Backup: 41 GB new or changed since 13 Oct (2 GPU checkpoints, data top-ups).
Connect the backup drive tonight; sm starts as soon as it appears.

When the drive appears, sm runs the backup at once with the existing restic setup, then messages you: "Backup done, 41 GB in 38 min, safe to unplug." The first full backup took 22 hours because it copied chain folders that can be rebuilt; under this rule those stay off the spinning drive.

Examples

1. A build, end to end

sm queue run --type background --label 4210-dataset --artifact 4210_dataset --disk 60G \
  --input 4180_labels --cwd ~/worktrees/research-4210 -- ./build.sh
# sm checks space, records commit and inputs, the job runs 22 min and writes 47 GB
sm artifact record 4210_dataset --describe @README.md --consumer #4211 --recipe-complete

--recipe-complete is the agent's word that re-running the recorded job at its commit rebuilds the folder; sm then tags that commit on GitHub so it survives the squash merge. While #4211 is open the folder is hot. Fourteen days after #4211 closes and nothing has used it, sm freezes it: 47 GB becomes about 1 GB. If a job names it again in March, the job waits about 30 seconds for the thaw.

2. A job that does not fit

The Mac has 300 GB free; the floor is 180 GB; a running job has declared 60 GB more. A new job declares 150 GB: 300 − 60 − 150 = 90, below the floor by 90 GB. sm moves an idle 193 GB folder to the cold store, which takes about a minute, and the job starts. Had the cold store been full too, sm would freeze idle folders there by score, and only then delete.

3. An unrecorded folder

An agent copies a 12 GB checkpoint, 4230_ckpt, back from a rented GPU by hand. Its name starts with 4230; the agent holding #4230 gets the record request and records it with --gpu-hours 6.5 --gpu-cost 14 and --consumer #4230. sm never deletes it, it is in the next backup, and it freezes after #4230 closes.

Already decided
  1. sm manages ~/artifacts; folders reach it through sm queue run --artifact, sm artifact new, or by appearing, after which sm asks for a record. (26 Sep, revised 15 Oct)
  2. A recipe is the queue job, its commit and the artifacts it read, recorded by sm. (26 Sep)
  3. A second, always-connected disk is the cold store. (15 Oct; replaces "no secondary drive")
  4. Cold folders are frozen with zstd proactively, not only when a disk is short. (15 Oct)
  5. Records carry consumers: the tickets that will read the folder. sm asks for the record when the folder appears and shows it in the sm app. (15 Oct)
  6. Deletion under pressure ranks by gigabytes freed per minute of rebuild; there is no 4-hour cutoff. (15 Oct)
  7. "Enough" free space is the largest single job output of the last 30 days. (15 Oct)
  8. Jobs declare the disk they will write, like --memory, and sm makes room or holds them. (15 Oct)
  9. Backups cover what cannot be rebuilt, plus ~/data, ~/projects and ~/.config; sm says when to connect the drive and runs the backup when it appears. (15 Oct)
  10. Runpod volumes are out of scope; no per-epic disk budgets, but totals by epic are shown. (26 Sep)

Risks

  • A folder read outside the queue gets no warning before it freezes. An agent working by hand finds FROZEN.md at the path, runs sm artifact thaw, and waits a minute or two. Nothing is lost.
  • A wrong "recipe complete" makes a deletion permanent. For example, the job used a binary built outside the queue. Mitigations: the report-only week; the agent instruction that recipe jobs build what they use; and deletion is the last resort, after moving and freezing.
  • The cold store is unplugged. Moved and frozen folders become unreadable and their paths fail loudly. sm checks the cold store is mounted before any move, freeze or thaw, and pauses all three, with one message to you, while it is missing.
  • GitHub cannot be reached. sm treats every consumer as still open, so nothing goes cold on that basis.

FAQ — what changed since the last revision

  • A second disk. An always-connected external SSD is the cold store; revision 1 ruled out a secondary drive.
  • Freezing replaces expiry. Nothing is deleted because its reason to exist lapsed; it is frozen. Deletion happens only under disk pressure or by an explicit Delete.
  • No 4-hour "cheap to rebuild" line. Deletion ranks by gigabytes per minute of rebuild, and moves and freezes come first.
  • Records are requested when a folder appears, and name consumers. Revision 1 deleted unregistered folders after 7 days; this revision freezes them (decision 1).
  • Review dates are gone (decision 6).
  • --est-size is now --disk, and a job's output can be placed on the cold store when the Mac is short.
  • Backups are new.

D. The sm artifact commands

All commands call the HTTP API (M.2), accept --root, resolve the root as: --root; else the root whose repo matches the current directory's GitHub repo; else the only root; else an error listing roots. Exit 0 on success; on refusal exit 2 with one line starting error:. #N means <root repo>#N; owner/repo#N is accepted.

D.1 sm artifact new <name> [--ticket N] [--epic L]

Creates a hand-made artifact in building at the placement E.3 would choose for 0 bytes (always the root unless it is below the floor) and prints its path. Refuses an invalid name, an existing record in any state but deleted, or an existing filesystem entry.

D.2 sm artifact record <name>... --describe TEXT|@FILE [--consumer #N]... [--project] [--keep-until DATE] [--recipe-complete] [--gpu-hours H] [--gpu-cost USD] [--ticket N] [--epic L]

  • Each named artifact must be building (no waiting or running job names it as output) or unrecorded. Description 1–8000 chars.
  • At least one of --consumer, --project, --keep-until is required. --keep-until must be after today.
  • --recipe-complete requires the names to be exactly one whole set, each member with at least one succeeded job, every succeeded job with non-NULL commit_sha and dirty = 0; then E.6. Any failure changes nothing. Not allowed on an unrecorded artifact (origin becomes adopted, never rebuildable).
  • ticket = --ticket, else the requester's single active claim in the root's repo (claims_for_session, work_claims.rs:872), else NULL. epic = --epic or NULL.
  • Success: state live, recorded_at, F runs for the set immediately.

Example error: error: 4210_dataset's set also contains 4210_context; name both with --recipe-complete.

D.3 sm artifact consumer <name> add|remove #N, sm artifact mark <name> --project|--no-project|--keep-until DATE|--no-keep-until

Edit a live or frozen record. Removing the last keep reason is allowed; F decides. Every change is an event; a new --project appears once in the digest.

D.4 sm artifact thaw <name> [--wait], sm artifact freeze <name>

thaw queues H.4 for the set and prints the ETA (data_bytes / thaw_rate); --wait blocks until done. freeze queues H.2 for a live set; refuses when in use, project, or keep_live_until in the future.

D.5 sm artifact delete <name> --reason TEXT

K.1 now, any state but deleted. Refuses when in use (names the job), project (remove the mark first), or an input of a non-deleted artifact (names the dependents).

D.6 sm artifact list [--state S] [--epic L] [--json], show <name>, disk

list: name, state, placement, hot/cold, disk/data/frozen size, consumers with state, rebuild cost ("22 min", "not rebuildable", "GPU"), backup due. show: the whole record, signals with evidence, recipe, last 20 events. disk: per filesystem size, free, floor, enough, outstanding reservations, jobs held on disk, cold-store mount state. --json emits the API objects.

E. Queue integration

E.1 Flags on sm queue run

Added to QueueRunArgs (bin/sm.rs:654) and the POST /queue-jobs body built in run_queue_run (bin/sm.rs:1731, body at :1840):

  • --artifact NAME (repeatable): output; absent (created building) or building. Refused if live, frozen or deleted ("choose a new name"), except K.2's own jobs.
  • --input NAME (repeatable): must be live, frozen or building-with-no-writer. Deleted and rebuildable: refused with "run sm artifact rebuild NAME first". Deleted and not rebuildable: refused.
  • --disk SIZE: parsed by parse_queue_memory_bytes (bin/sm.rs:5619). Required with --artifact, except K.2 jobs, which use the recorded bytes_written.

A job with none of these behaves exactly as today. --input alone marks inputs in use for the job's life.

E.2 At submission (create_queue_job, http.rs:8108 → create_queue_job_conn, queue.rs:4091)

  1. Resolve the root from cwd (D rules).
  2. Detect inputs: search argv and the script file text (≤ 1 MiB, else refuse) case-insensitively for the root path in three spellings (absolute, ~/…, $HOME/…) and for the cold-store path, each followed by /NAME with the next character outside [A-Za-z0-9_.-]. A NAME that is a recorded artifact or alias (resolved) and not an output is an input with source = detected. A detected deleted input does not refuse; the requester gets [sm artifact] job <label> references deleted artifact <name>.
  3. Capture git state in cwd (top level, HEAD, status --porcelain --untracked-files=no non-empty ⇒ dirty) via a shared git_in(dir, args) helper.
  4. In one transaction with the queue row: create absent outputs (placement decided at E.3; the folder is created at start), insert the reservation, artifact_jobs rows (state queued), artifact_inputs, and recompute set_id.
  5. For each input that is frozen: queue a thaw (H.4) now, so it overlaps the wait.

E.3 Admission (admit_pending_queue_jobs_conn, queue.rs:4439)

Applies only to jobs with a reservation; runs after the existing checks pass. In order:

  1. Thaw hold: any input frozen or thawing ⇒ holding reason thawing. (New reason; add to queue_hold_explanation, queue.rs:7769.)
  2. Disk, with statvfs from nix::sys::statvfs (feature fs already on): 
    free(fs)        = f_bavail × f_frsize
floor(fs)       = floor_percent/100 × f_blocks × f_frsize
outstanding(fs) = Σ running reservations r placed on fs: max(0, r.disk − (current_output_size(r) − r.start_bytes))
fits(fs)        = free − outstanding − job.disk ≥ floor
    • If fits(root fs): place on root.
    • Else run J for the root fs with needed = floor − (free − outstanding − job.disk) (at most once per 10 min per job); if it now fits, place on root.
    • Else if the cold store is mounted and fits(cold fs): place on cold (I.3).
    • Else hold with reason disk_space, set first_disk_hold_at, and let J step 4 message the owner.

When the job starts, its outputs are created at their placement and start_bytes = current output size. current_output_size uses the size walk (G.2), cached 5 min per job. A job whose current reason is disk_space or thawing expires disk_hold_max_wait_hours after its first such hold, not at the normal window (expire_pending_queue_jobs_conn, queue.rs:4675); its wake names the reason. Perf rules run first and are unchanged.

E.4 While running

Outputs and inputs of queued and running jobs carry the in use signal (F). If current_output_size − start_bytes > overrun_factor × disk, the requester gets one message [sm artifact] <label> has written X, declared Y and an overrun event; nothing else happens.

E.5 At completion (finish_queue_job_conn_with_policy, queue.rs:7425)

Update artifact_jobs (state, exit code, wall_seconds), measure outputs, set bytes_written, delete the reservation, and set last_used_at = started_at on every input and output. Failed, timed-out and cancelled jobs stay as history and do not count toward the recipe. The recording shares the completion path, so the existing retry of unnotified completions repairs a crash between them.

E.6 Commit tags

On record --recipe-complete, for each distinct commit_sha of the set's succeeded jobs, tag artifact/<set_id>/<sha7>: skip if git ls-remote --tags origin shows it; else push <sha>:refs/tags/<tag> from the job's cwd if it exists, else from the mirror (F.3); if neither has the commit, fail with error: commit <sha7> is not available to push; rebuild from a pushed commit. When a set becomes deleted and not rebuildable, or is rebuilt at a new commit, remove old tags from the mirror; failures are logged and retried nightly.

F.2 When

At nightly_time for all artifacts; at the start of any pressure pass if older than one hour; immediately for artifacts touched by record, consumer, mark. In use is always computed live.

F.3 Named on main

A bare mirror per repo at <mirror_dir>/<owner>__<name>.git, created with git clone --bare --filter=blob:none, updated with git fetch origin '+refs/heads/<branch>:refs/heads/<branch>' nightly; never touches repo_checkout. One pass: git -C <mirror> grep -I -i -o -E '<root-final-segment>/[A-Za-z0-9][A-Za-z0-9_.-]*' <branch> -- . ':!docs/working/' ':!docs/execution/' (exclusions from named_exclude). The last path segment of each match that names an artifact or alias adds a named row with the file as evidence (≤ 20 per artifact).

F.4 Nightly actions after evaluation

In enforcing mode, freeze every cold live set that is not in use, in descending disk_bytes, at most max_concurrent_ops at a time, skipping all of it while the cold store is unmounted. In report-only mode, log would_freeze.

G.1 The one-minute scan

A dedicated thread in the main.rs idiom (run_background_pass, main.rs:29) reads only the top level of each root every scan_seconds. No filesystem-event dependency is added: events would not name the writer either, and a top-level read_dir of a few hundred entries costs microseconds.

G.2 Size walk (nightly)

Per live artifact, walk with lstat, never following symlinks or crossing st_dev; count each (st_dev, st_ino) once. disk_bytes = Σ st_blocks × 512; data_bytes = Σ st_size of regular files; content_stamp = "<files>:<data_bytes>:<max mtime>" over regular files excluding any named .DS_Store (Finder writes these into any folder a person opens; counting them would make every opened folder look changed). A walk competes for I/O, so it waits while a perf job runs or waits for its window, unless it has not run for 36 hours.

  2. The name matches ^(\d{2,6})[_-] ⇒ the active claim holder for (root repo, number). Add WorkClaimStore::active_holder(repo, number) using the existing predicate ended_at IS NULL AND reserved_at IS NULL (work_claims/checks.rs:304).

No live attributed session ⇒ the artifact appears in the digest under "Needs you: unrecorded, no owner" with Delete and Thaw/Keep actions. At grace_until (initially initial_grace_days for entries present at the first scan) it is frozen (A) unless in use.

H. Freeze and thaw

H.1 Format

A frozen artifact is the directory <cold_store>/.frozen/<name>/ containing manifest.json and parts part-00001.tar.zst, … New crate dependencies: tar and zstd (feature zstdmt). No external zstd or tar binary is required to freeze or thaw, but the format is plain: any machine with zstd -d | tar -x can read a part.

  • Parts: regular files, symlinks and directories in byte-wise path order, packed greedily into parts of at most freeze_part_bytes of input; a file larger than that is a part by itself. Parts let thaw run in parallel and bound the damage of one bad sector.
  • Each part: a PAX tar stream (paths relative to the artifact, mode, mtime, symlinks stored as symlinks, no ownership) compressed with zstd at freeze_level, frame content checksum on, multithreaded with max(1, cores / freeze_parallel_parts) workers.
  • manifest.json: {"name","frozen_at","sm_version","level","data_bytes","file_count","content_stamp","parts":[{"file","input_bytes","output_bytes","first","last"}],"entries":[{"path","kind","size","mtime","mode","target"}]}, entries excluding .DS_Store.

Measured on a 128.3 GB data-pipeline folder on the target SSD: level 3 gave 16.3× in 50 s, level 9 gave 19.5× in 47 s (the input read limits both); decompression ran at 3.1 and 3.7 GB/s. Level 9 is the default. Ratio is content-bound: float32 .npy summaries sampled at 1.1×, labels at 1.1–1.2×, pipeline chains at 18–43×.

H.2 Freeze

  1. Preconditions (re-checked in the transaction at step 6): state live, not in use, cold store mounted, cold-store free ≥ data_bytes / ratio_estimate × 1.2 + floor. Set op = freezing.
  2. Remove .DS_Store files from the tree. Walk it to build the entry list and record t0.
  3. Write parts to <cold_store>/.frozen/.<name>.freezing/, up to freeze_parallel_parts at once; fsync each part and the directory.
  4. Verify: decompress each part (the zstd frame checksum verifies content), parse its tar headers, and compare paths, kinds, sizes and symlink targets with the manifest. Walk the live tree again: any entry added, removed, resized or with mtime > t0 (excluding .DS_Store) ⇒ abort, delete the parts, event op_failed (reason changed during freeze), retry next night.
  5. Rename the directory to <cold_store>/.frozen/<name>/.
  6. In one transaction: re-check not in use; state frozen, frozen_bytes, frozen_at, op = NULL. Then swap the path (H.3) and delete the live tree (K.1 mechanics, tolerant of .DS_Store reappearing: on ENOTEMPTY remove .DS_Store and retry once).

H.3 The frozen placeholder

The root path becomes a real directory (replacing a symlink if the artifact lived on the cold store) containing only FROZEN.md:

# <name> is frozen
Frozen <local date> by sm. <data size> of data in <frozen size> of archives.
Thaw: sm artifact thaw <name> --wait      (about <ETA>)
Queue jobs that name this folder thaw it automatically.
Record: sm artifact show <name>

The swap is: rename the old entry to <root>/.<name>.old, create the placeholder at <root>/.<name>.placeholder, rename it to <root>/<name>, then delete .<name>.old.

H.4 Thaw

  1. Preconditions: state frozen, cold store mounted, cold-store free − floor ≥ manifest data_bytes. Else the requester (or digest) is told why and the thaw waits for J on the cold store. Set op = thawing.
  2. Extract parts in parallel (thaw_parallel_parts) into <cold_store>/.<name>.thawing/, restoring mode and mtime; refuse any entry path that is absolute or contains ...
  3. Verify the extracted tree against the manifest (paths, kinds, sizes, targets).
  4. Rename to <cold_store>/<name>; swap the placeholder for a symlink (same rename dance as H.3); delete .frozen/<name>; in one transaction: state live, location cold, thawed_at = last_used_at = now, op = NULL.
  5. Re-run admission for jobs held on thawing.

Thaw rate for estimates is the median of the last five thaws' data_bytes / seconds, default 100 GB/min until five exist.

H.5 Crash recovery

On startup, for each artifact with op set: freezing ⇒ delete .frozen/.<name>.freezing, clear op; thawing ⇒ delete .<name>.thawing, clear op; moving ⇒ I.2 rule; deleting ⇒ finish K.1. Any <root>/.<name>.old whose artifact's path is valid is deleted; one whose path is missing is renamed back.

I. Moves between disks

I.1 When

Only as a pressure action on the root filesystem (J), or as output placement (I.3). Never for an artifact in use, never while the cold store is unmounted.

I.2 Move a live artifact from the root to the cold store

  1. op = moving. Remove .DS_Store files. Copy the tree to <cold_store>/.<name>.moving/ in Rust: regular files with fcopyfile/copyfile(3) using COPYFILE_DATA | COPYFILE_STAT | COPYFILE_XATTR so macOS transparent compression and extended attributes survive; symlinks recreated; names beginning ._ copied as ordinary files (the macOS ditto tool silently folds them into metadata, which loses them).
  2. Verify: the destination's (path, kind, size, target) list equals the source's, both excluding .DS_Store. Any difference ⇒ delete the copy, event op_failed.
  3. Rename to <cold_store>/<name>; rename the source to <root>/.<name>.old; create the symlink <root>/<name> → <cold_store>/<name>; location cold, op = NULL; delete .<name>.old with the .DS_Store retry.

Crash recovery (H.5 moving): if the symlink exists, finish step 3; else delete the partial copy and keep the source.

I.3 Output placement on the cold store

When E.3 places a job's output on the cold store, sm creates <cold_store>/<name> and the symlink <root>/<name> before the job starts, and records location cold. The job writes through the symlink. Reservation placed = cold makes its outstanding bytes count against the cold store.

I.4 Cold store missing

If the cold-store path does not exist or its st_dev equals the root's (not mounted), sm performs no freeze, thaw or move, holds jobs whose inputs are frozen with reason thawing, places no output there, and sends the owner one message per 6 hours: "The cold store is not connected; N frozen and M moved artifacts are unreadable."

J. The pressure pass and the score

J.1 Triggers and target

Every pressure_check_seconds per filesystem (root fs and cold fs) when free < enough(fs); and from E.3 with its needed. One pass per filesystem at a time; a trigger during a pass waits and re-measures.

  • enough(root fs) = max(largest bytes_written of one succeeded queue job finishing in the last enough_window_days, floor).
  • enough(cold fs) = max(largest data_bytes among frozen artifacts, floor), so any one artifact can thaw.
  • Stop rule: free − outstanding ≥ max(enough, floor + needed_by_waiting_jobs), where needed_by_waiting_jobs is the largest disk among jobs held on disk_space for this fs.

J.2 Candidates and score

Each candidate is (action, set) with freed bytes on this filesystem and wait minutes if needed again; score = freed_GB / max(wait_min, 0.1); a move has score +∞. Ties: larger freed, then name.

J.3 Pass

  1. Refresh F if older than one hour; measure free.
  2. Build candidates, sort by score descending, and execute in order (moves and freezes through the op worker, at most max_concurrent_ops), re-measuring after each completion and stopping at the stop rule. Report-only: log would_* and assume the space freed.

K. Deletion and rebuild

K.1 Deletion mechanics

  • Targets are only <root>/<name>, <cold_store>/<name>, <cold_store>/.frozen/<name> and sm's own dot-prefixed temporaries; name is a validated single segment; roots are canonicalized once at startup and any target whose parent is not exactly one of them is refused.
  • Walk without following symlinks; a symlink inside an artifact is removed as a link. Then remove every alias of the artifact. On ENOTEMPTY remove .DS_Store and retry once.
  • Immediately before removal, re-check in one transaction that the state still justifies deletion and the set is not in use.
  • Partial failure: state unchanged, event op_failed, digest "Needs you"; no retry within the day.
  • Deleting leaves the record with deleted_at, deleted_by (sm | session id | owner), delete_reason (pressure | agent | owner | orphan | vanished), and rebuildable.

K.2 Rebuild

sm artifact rebuild <name> accepts a deleted, rebuildable set; refuses if any input is deleted (naming it) . Steps: thaw frozen inputs (H.4); create a detached worktree per commit at ~/worktrees/sm-rebuild-<set_id>-<sha7> from repo_checkout after fetching the tag; set members building; queue the set's succeeded jobs in original seq order, each with its original type, timeout, env, argv and script text (written to <worktree>/.sm-rebuild/<seq>.sh), cwd = <worktree>/<cwd_rel>, the same --artifact outputs and disk = bytes_written, chained by the completion path. On success: members live with their previous consumers, marks and description; event rebuilt; worktree removed; requester told [sm artifact] rebuilt <set> in N min. On failure: chain stops, members stay building, worktree kept, event rebuild_failed, digest "Needs you", and the set is excluded from delete candidates until a later rebuild succeeds.

L. Backup

L.1 Scope

  • Static paths from config (static_paths), stamped nightly with the G.2 stamp into backup_static.
  • Artifacts: every recorded artifact in state live or frozen that is not rebuildable (no complete recipe, or any GPU hours/spend), plus unrecorded artifacts. A live one contributes its resolved directory; a frozen one contributes <cold_store>/.frozen/<name>.
  • Due: its current stamp differs from backup_stamp (for frozen ones the stamp is the manifest's frozen_at).

L.2 Nudge

Nightly: if anything is due and the oldest due item has been due for ≥ nudge_after_days, and no backup nudge was sent in the last nudge_min_interval_days, the digest carries a Backup section (not blocking): the total due size, counts by kind, and "Connect the backup drive; sm starts as soon as it appears."

L.3 Running

  1. A thread checks every 60 s whether backup.volume exists and is a mount point (its st_dev differs from its parent's).
  2. On a transition to present, if anything is due, or the last successful run is older than 24 h and anything is due: write the include list (absolute resolved paths, one per line, static paths first) to ~/.local/share/claude-sessions/backup/include-<run id>.txt; record each item's current stamp as its pending stamp; submit a queue job --type background --label sm-backup --timeout none running backup.command with environment SM_BACKUP_INCLUDE=<file> and SM_BACKUP_VOLUME=<volume>; insert a backup_runs row.
  3. On success: every included item's backup_stamp = its pending stamp and backed_up_at = start time; owner message (not blocking): "Backup done: <bytes> in <duration>. Safe to unplug." On failure: owner message with the last 20 log lines; stamps unchanged.
  4. A second appearance of the volume while a run is queued or running does nothing.

The command owns the tool choice. The first user's command runs rsync for its data directory and restic (repository readable by rustic too) with --files-from "$SM_BACKUP_INCLUDE" for the rest, then its retention policy.

Z. FAQ

Why not macOS transparent compression instead of freezing? It needs no thaw, but it uses older, block-limited compressors (about 2× on the same pipeline folders that zstd shrinks 16–19×) and cannot compress a file over 2 GiB, which is where most bytes live. It also comes undone whenever a job rewrites a file. sm preserves it when moving folders (I.2) and otherwise leaves it alone.

Why not a compressed disk image per folder? Mounting dozens of images, keeping them mounted across restarts, and their read-only nature cost more than a minute's thaw, and their compression is weaker than zstd's.

Why thaw onto the cold store, not the Mac? Reads through the symlink run at SSD speed, and a thaw must never be the thing that fills the Mac. A job that writes the folder after a thaw writes there too.

Why a placeholder directory rather than nothing at the path? An agent reading by hand gets "No such file" for a missing path and has no clue why. A folder holding only FROZEN.md explains itself on ls.

Why 16 GiB parts? Parallel thaw (one stream of zstd decompresses at about 3.5 GB/s; several reach the disk's write speed), and one bad sector costs one part.

Why poll the top level instead of watching filesystem events? Events on macOS do not name the writing process, so they would not help attribution; a one-minute poll of a few hundred names is negligible and adds no dependency.

Why must the cold store be APFS? exFAT, the usual format for drives shared with Windows, cannot hold symlinks or permissions and stores macOS metadata as extra ._name files, one of which collides with a folder of the same name. Moves, placeholders and symlinked outputs all depend on APFS.

Why does a thawed folder count as used? Otherwise a thaw for a job that then fails fast would be frozen again that night, and the next attempt would pay the thaw again.

Why are frozen folders deleted only under pressure? They cost a twentieth of their size, and a deleted folder costs its rebuild. The score already puts "delete frozen" far below "freeze" (Memo, section 3); deleting them on a schedule would trade a few gigabytes for rebuild minutes nobody asked to spend.

What happened to holds and review dates? Holds became consumers (tickets), --project and --keep-until; the automatic signals (named on main, in use, input of) remain. Review dates are dropped (Memo, decision 6) because a frozen folder no longer needs a periodic human decision.

Can a job write outside its declared outputs? Not under sm's control: sm does not sandbox jobs. A new top-level folder is caught by the one-minute scan and requested; growth inside another artifact shows in its size and is not acted on.

Worktrees? Out of scope: sm worktree keep governs them.
