2170 — Auto PM: sm runs its own product manager

Author: Claude Opus 5.5 8bbcb31a · Ticket #2170 · 2026-10-15

Memo

Build it, in four tickets, and switch it on with a weekly allowance you can cancel. sm starts one standing product-manager agent on Opus 5.5. It studies what you actually do across the Android app, the web app, docs and your agent sessions. It proposes one change at a time, each with a success test fixed before the change ships. A Codex reviewer attacks each plan before anything is built. Then it ships the change behind a switch that turns it off in under a second, measures it, and keeps or reverts it. A change it cannot undo on its own, it builds and measures in a shadow copy of sm, then asks you a single yes or no. It records what worked and what did not in one file it keeps. Beyond that, it costs you no attention.

Three facts make this worth doing now:

  • You are sm's product manager, and sm learns nothing from watching you.
  • Reading your habits off stored state goes wrong. In one session I misread you twice: I took unmarked messages for a backlog, and a quiet web inbox for a dead feature. You work from docs, and use the Android inbox as an alert. A PM must learn from behaviour, by surface, and test before acting.
  • Quota expires unused most weeks. Claude's weekly meter peaked at 39–92% over the last eight weeks, so 40+ points usually expire; Codex peaked at 17–90%. A PM funded mostly from that surplus pays for itself from day 0.
Auto PM
One standing sm agent with the role autopm. It decides what to change in sm and proves whether the change helped. It writes plans and measures results; separate agents build each change.
Cycle
One week of auto PM work. sm opens a cycle ticket under goal #2170 every Monday, and the cycle closes when the auto PM publishes that week's readout.
Owner activity log
A new record, kept locally, of what you do in sm: which surface, which screen, which action, and whether you came from a push notification. It stores routes, ids and times, never message or prompt text.
Experiment
One change with a hypothesis, a metric, a baseline, a threshold fixed in advance, and a date by which it is measured.
Switch
An on/off setting that sm reads live, with no restart. Every behaviour change the auto PM ships sits behind one; off means exactly today's behaviour.
Revertable
sm can undo the change by itself with nothing lost: turn the switch off, or revert the commit and redeploy.
Shadow mode
Building a change on a branch and running it against a throwaway copy of sm's live state, without touching the real server or app, to gather evidence before asking you.
Playbook
The one file the auto PM keeps (docs/autopm/playbook.md): how you work, what worked, what did not, and its standing rules.
Points
Percentage points of a provider's weekly meter, the same number the sm app's usage meter shows. "20 Codex points" means 20% of a week of Codex.

How a change moves through the loop

The board carries all of it. #2170 is the goal and its lane is the autopm lane. Each Monday sm opens a cycle ticket under it and wakes the auto PM. The auto PM files each experiment's build tickets under the cycle ticket. sm auto-starts those tickets within budget, one fresh agent per ticket, in the normal workflow: PR, review, merge on a clean review, restart. The auto PM stays one seat for weeks. sm's default context handoff hands it to a fresh successor when its context fills, and its role, reminders and claims move with it.

What it sees

Today sm keeps your doc opens, message reads and replies, and agent time and tokens. It does not keep which surface you used, which screen, or whether a push brought you there. Ticket 1 adds a local activity log of exactly that: routes, ids, screens and timing, never message or prompt text (Appendix A has the before/after table). Typing into the Claude app and typing in a terminal look the same to sm, and stay that way.

Your own tickets and lane order are its strongest signal of what you want; it never works where one of your open tickets does.

Two automatic safeties sit under all of it. A health guard in the server turns off every switch flipped in the last hour if owner-facing errors or failed sm send deliveries jump. A kill switch in Settings turns every switch off and stops the auto PM. One thumbs-down on a change reverts it at once, without waiting for the auto PM. A switch turned off by the guard, the kill switch or a thumbs-down stays off: the auto PM cannot turn it back on; only you can. sm itself refuses to build or switch on anything whose plan has not merged after review, so the review step is enforced, not just promised.

How it knows a change helped

Every plan fixes four things before anything is built: the metric, as a query committed to the repo; its baseline over the previous 14 days; the bar to clear; and the measurement date. The default bar is a 25% improvement in the median, holding in both weeks of a 14-day window, while guard metrics do not get 25% worse. The guard metrics are your manual reverts, thumbs-down and bug reports about the area. With one user and noisy weeks, the bar is deliberately high: it detects big wins and ignores small ones. A change that misses the bar is reverted, even if it "might be helping". sm runs the measurement itself, from the committed query, so the auto PM cannot grade its own work.

Thumbs are optional, and silence counts as neutral. The app gets a small "What changed" card listing live changes with 👍 / 👎. 👎 reverts immediately.

The auto PM is measured too. Each readout reports changes kept against changes tried, points spent per kept change, and your thumbs. After four straight cycles with no kept change, sm pauses the auto PM and tells you once.

How it learns

The playbook is one Markdown file in git, capped at 1,500 lines, about 20,000 tokens: roughly 2% of the auto PM's context, read once per cycle and once per handoff. It holds conclusions, not data. Each entry is a few lines that link to the plan doc and measurement record behind it, so the evidence never has to fit in the file. It has six fixed sections: how you work (observed patterns, each with evidence and date), what worked, what did not, standing rules, its own method, and ranked open hypotheses. The auto PM reads it at the start of every cycle, and every handoff brief points its successor to it. It changes only through the cycle's readout PR, so you can read or edit it like any file. It starts from what this session learned:

  • You work from docs, not the inbox.
  • On Android, the inbox is an alert for things you are expecting.
  • Asks that need judgment on a long artifact stall, as #2114 in fractal-algo-rust shows.
  • Your attention is the scarcest currency.
  • Never infer disuse from stored state.

Two worked examples

Heavy asks. Hypothesis: asks that require reading a long artifact wait several times longer than yes/no asks. #2114 has waited since 2026-10-14 on a single three-way call about a day plan. Change: when an agent asks you to judge a doc, the doc reader opens with a decision card: the choices, the agent's recommendation in two lines, and one button each. Metric: median hours from ask to decision for judgment asks, from the activity log and doc review records. Bar: at least 25% faster across 14 days, with no rise in reversed decisions. Path: doc reader, behind a switch, no install. It ships alone.

Inbox alerts. It first checks two weeks of the activity log for the pattern you described: Android inbox opens cluster just after pushes about things you asked for. If the pattern holds, it gives those pushes their own class and measures minutes from push to open. If not, the playbook records that and nothing ships.

Budget

Each provider's allowance is a dedicated amount plus surplus. The surplus is quota the projection says will expire, released only from day four of the week and only beyond a 20-point safety margin. sm counts the auto PM's spend across its own seat and successors, the agents building its tickets, and the reviews of their PRs. Each build ticket reserves its estimated cost when it starts, and sm refuses a start whose estimate exceeds what is left unreserved. Overshoot is therefore limited to estimation error. At zero, agents mid-ticket reach a safe stopping point and stop. "Skip this week" in Settings sets that week's allowance to zero. The auto PM's own thinking runs on Claude. Building prefers Codex, which has the most unused quota.

What it costs you

  • Once: reviewing this memo, switching it on, and installing one app update.
  • Weekly, optional: a one-page readout among your docs, with no notification.
  • At most one message a week, only for shadow-built changes: up to three yes/no decisions, each saying what the shadow run could and could not test. No answer within 14 days means no.

A.2 Surface.

  • android: the request passed device_auth_user (http.rs) or arrived through the MobileApp Access application (mark_in_app in http.rs). Owner pages loaded in the app's WebView (ui/watch/DocReader.kt) count as android.
  • web: the request passed owner_web_guard (http.rs), browser Access login or sm's Google session cookie, and not the above.
  • agent_prompt: a Claude UserPromptSubmit hook or a Codex prompt the server classes as owner-typed (is_owner_typed_prompt in owner_messages.rs; Codex path codex_fork_user_prompt in sessions.rs). target_kind=session, target_id = the session. The Claude app, claude.ai and the terminal are not distinguishable; record them all as agent_prompt.
  • terminal: submitted input on the app terminal websocket (the two owner_answered(…, "terminal") calls in http.rs, gated by terminal_frame_submits_input). Coalesce to one event per session per 60 s of input.

Requests from agents (an x-sm-session-id header or the local-agent gateway) and loopback requests without owner auth are never recorded.

A.3 Action. action is a stable name from a static map keyed by method and route template, for example GET /client/sessions/{id} → session.open, GET /docs/{id} → doc.open, POST /inbox/…/reply → inbox.reply, PUT /client/board/order → board.order. A route absent from the map records its template string, such as GET /client/queue, so nothing is silently dropped. Keep the map in one module and cover it with a test that every owner route resolves to some action. Ids from path parameters fill target_id. Query strings and bodies are never stored.

A.4 Background requests are not events. A request is background when it carries x-sm-bg: 1, or when no client header is present and its route is on the poll list: /watch/state, /client/host-status, /client/board/badge, /client/utilization/series, /client/usage/meters, /client/queue/stats. Static subresources are also background: /assets/*, and non-page files served beside a doc, such as images. Background requests are not stored.

A.4a Coalescing. A request with the same (surface, device_id, action, target_kind, target_id) as a row written for that device in the last 60 s writes no new row, unless it carries x-sm-screen or x-sm-nav. Polls from clients that do not yet send x-sm-bg therefore leave at most one row a minute per open screen.

A.5 Client headers (ticket 4). Android adds x-sm-screen (the current screen's route name, for example board, session/detail, inbox/thread, doc) and x-sm-bg in its OkHttp interceptor (AuthInterceptor.kt). It sends x-sm-nav: push:<notice_id> on the first request after a notification tap; the server stores that as nav=push and notice_id=<notice_id>. Any other header value outside the four names stores nav=NULL. The app sends launch after a cold start, link after opening an sm link from outside the app, and nav otherwise. The web client sends the same headers from its router (crates/sm-server/src/web/ui.js).

A.6 Retention. Rows older than 180 days are deleted by a daily prune. The log is never sent off the machine.

A.7 Read. GET /autopm/events?since_ms=&until_ms=&surface=&action=&limit=&cursor= (owner or autopm role). Rows are ordered by (at_ms, id); limit ≤ 10 000. The response is {events, next_cursor}: next_cursor is the last row's "<at_ms>:<id>", or null at the end, and cursor= continues strictly after it. since_ms is inclusive and until_ms exclusive. limit defaults to 1 000, and a value outside 1–10 000 returns 400. CLI: sm autopm events [--since 7d] [--surface S] [--action A] [--json] follows cursors to the end.

B.1 Switches.

CREATE TABLE experiment_switches (

  • The server holds an in-memory copy, updated on every write, and code asks switches::is_on("key"). A missing key reads as off.
  • When autopm.enabled is false (C.1), every switch reads as off whatever its row says.
  • Only POST /autopm/experiments creates a switch row, with on_=0, whenever the experiment has a switch_key (every path=switch experiment, and any shadow experiment whose post-approval build has a switch). The key must not already exist. An experiment can therefore only flip the switch it created.
  • Clients receive switches: {key: bool} in /client/bootstrap and /client/settings; every response also carries x-sm-switches: <version>, a counter that rises on every switch write (and on enable or disable). A client that sees a version other than its cached one re-reads the switches before acting on its next screen. In the foreground, both clients also re-read at least every 30 s on their existing refresh loops. An off transition therefore reaches an open app within 30 s, or at its next request.
  • Writes: PUT /autopm/switches/{key} {on, reason}.
    • Owner: may set either value. Turning a switch on as owner clears held. If its experiment is reverted and autopm.enabled is true, the same transaction moves it back to live. While Auto PM is disabled, turning on a reverted experiment's switch is refused with 409 autopm_disabled; turn Auto PM on first. On a successful re-enable: a fresh live_at_ms and measure_at_ms, extended=0, baseline_until_ms unchanged, its old measurement rows kept but superseded. For any revert-cleanup ticket for it, sm does three things in the same action: places a merge hold (the existing merge_holds) on every open PR linked to that ticket; closes the ticket with the comment "Rajesh re-enabled the change"; and, if an agent holds its claim, sends it [sm autopm] cleanup cancelled: Rajesh re-enabled the change. Close your PR unmerged and stop. An auto-start row still waiting is cancelled. sm also sets autopm_fixes.cancelled_at on the cleanup ticket, and whenever it later sees a PR linked to a cancelled cleanup ticket (by sm pr, a Closes #N reference, or board PR sync), it places a merge hold on that PR at once. A PR opened after the re-enable is therefore held too. The only other reverted → live path is this owner action.
    • The autopm role: may turn its own experiment's switch off at any time. It may turn it on only when every switch-on gate in B.3 holds. A held switch refuses it with 409 held: only Rajesh can turn this back on.
  • Off must equal pre-change behaviour exactly. Every experiment's build PR includes a test asserting that.

B.3 States and gates. planned → building → dark → live → measuring → kept | reverted. Proposal experiments run planned → awaiting_owner → abandoned; on yes, sm files the issue in target_repo first and writes the note. Each proposal decision in the ask names target_repo. Shadow experiments run planned → building → shadowed → awaiting_owner, then on yes → building → dark → …, and on no (or 14 days without an answer) → abandoned. Only pre-live states (planned, building, shadowed, awaiting_owner, dark) may go to abandoned, with a note. A live, measuring or kept experiment can leave only by reverted, which turns its switch off in the same transaction. Two direct transitions are server-only: live | measuring → reverted when the guard, the kill switch, a 👎 or your own switch-off turns the switch off; and kept → reverted when a 👎 or your switch-off lands during the 30-day period before cleanup (F.2). The decision note names the cause. dark means every build PR in tickets_json has merged, and the running server build (its x-sm-build commit) contains their merge commits. For Android changes, the app must also report a client_version at or above the build that carries them. A switched experiment's switch is still off. sm moves building → dark itself when these hold; live means switched on; measuring begins at measure_at_ms. Routes: POST /autopm/experiments, PATCH /autopm/experiments/{id} (state moves only along the arrows), GET /autopm/experiments; CLI sm autopm exp {new|set|list|show}.

sm enforces these gates server-side. Each refusal returns 409 with the failing gate's name.

B.4 Health guard.

  • Samples. The server keeps three in-memory counters: owner-route responses with status ≥ 500, failed sm send deliveries, and caught handler panics. At each minute boundary it writes them, then resets them, into guard_samples(minute_ms INTEGER PRIMARY KEY, owner_5xx INTEGER, delivery_failures INTEGER, panics INTEGER) in utilization.db. Minutes the server was down have no row. Rows older than 8 days are pruned.
  • Current value. Per counter, the sum over the rows of the latest 10 minutes. Missing rows count as zero.
  • Baseline. For every minute m from 7 days ago up to 60 minutes ago whose 10 rows m−9…m all exist, take the sum of those rows. The baseline is the 95th percentile of those sums, by nearest rank.
  • Threshold. max(3, 2 × baseline). With fewer than 1 440 baseline sums (under a day of history, as right after first deploy), the threshold is 3: the guard trips more easily, never less.
  • Action. The guard is evaluated every 60 s. When any counter's current value exceeds its threshold, the guard does two things. It turns off every switch turned on within the last 60 minutes, with held=1, changed_by=guard. Then it wakes the auto PM with [sm autopm] guard reverted <keys>: <counter> <value> > <threshold>. Switches on for longer than 60 minutes are left alone; those failures are the auto PM's to diagnose.

B.5 Kill switch. autopm.enabled=false (Settings › Auto PM, or PUT /client/settings) takes effect immediately:

  1. In one transaction, every switch with on_=1 becomes on_=0, held=1, changed_by=killswitch.
  2. sm stops auto-starting autopm-lane tickets.
  3. Cycle creation stops.
  4. The auto PM and every agent on an autopm ticket get [sm autopm] disabled by Rajesh: push work in progress, comment on your ticket, stop.

Re-enabling turns nothing back on. Held switches stay off until you turn each one on yourself.

B.6 Bug fixes and manual rollback. A bug fix with no lasting side effects is filed with POST /autopm/issues {kind: "fix"}. It needs no experiment and no plan, but does need a budget reservation, and it ships through the ordinary PR and review flow. Every unattended live experiment has a switch, so the guard, the kill switch and 👎 always have something to turn off. Manual rollback, used for fixes and for switchless shadow changes you approved: git revert the merge commit, open a PR, wait for a clean review, merge, then scripts/restart-rust-server.sh. If main has moved on in a way that conflicts, deploy the previous build with --allow-behind-main first, then land the revert. For an approved data change, run the restore procedure tested in shadow mode (E.1). kind: "fix" requires the ticket body to state "side effects: none".

C.2 Allowance per provider per weekly window (Claude weekly_all; Codex codex_10080 of the account the build agents use):

allowance = 0                               if skip_window_starting = this window's start
          = dedicated                       before 96 h into the window
          = dedicated + min(cap, max(0, headroom - margin))   from 96 h on
headroom  = projected_free_headroom_points from build_projection (usage_report.rs)
            -- if build_projection returns None, or its newest sample is older than 6 h,
            -- the surplus term is 0: allowance = dedicated (or 0 when skipped)

Example: on Thursday the Claude projection says the week ends at 55%, so headroom is 45. The allowance is 5 + min(15, 45 − 20) = 20 points.

C.3 Spend. Spend is the token use in usage.db seat_tokens for the current window, converted to points with the fitted rates in quota_rates.rs. It covers three groups of seats:

  1. Every seat that has held the autopm role, including handoff successors.
  2. Every seat that has claimed a ticket under #2170.
  3. Every review run on an autopm PR: plan PRs, build and fix PRs, and readout PRs, by any provider in the fallback chain.

The PM's in-context research helpers count as part of its seat. GET /autopm/budget returns {window_start, resets_at, providers: {claude: {allowance, spent, reserved, remaining, pm: {held, spent, left}, reviews: {held, spent, left}}, codex: {…}}}. Here reserved is open_hold (C.4), and remaining = allowance − spent − reserved is the balance for new tickets. pm.left and reviews.left are what each standing reservation still has; CLI sm autopm budget.

C.4 Enforcement. Build tickets reserve budget when they start:

CREATE TABLE autopm_reservations (
  repo TEXT NOT NULL, number INTEGER NOT NULL,
  provider TEXT NOT NULL, points REAL NOT NULL,
  window_start TEXT NOT NULL,
  created_at_ms INTEGER NOT NULL, released_at_ms INTEGER,
  PRIMARY KEY (repo, number, provider, window_start)
);
  • PUT /autopm/auto-start carries points_est > 0 for the build provider, which is required. Reviews are not reserved per ticket; they draw on the standing review reservation below.
  • Admission runs in one BEGIN IMMEDIATE transaction, so concurrent starts serialize. 
    open_hold    = Σ over unreleased reservations r of this provider and window:
                 max(0, r.points − spent_by_ticket(r))
unreserved   = allowance − spent − open_hold
admit iff points_est ≤ unreserved(build provider)
  • A reservation is released when its ticket closes, or when no live session holds the ticket's claim any more. A handoff moves the claim to the successor, so the reservation stays.
  • When a ticket's own spend passes 1.5 × its reservation, sm sends that ticket's agent [sm autopm] ticket over estimate: reach a safe stop.
  • When a provider's max(allowance, Σ rollover points admitted this window) − spent reaches zero, sm sends the same safe-stop message to the auto PM and to every agent on an autopm ticket of that provider. A safe stop means: push the work, comment on the ticket what remains, then go idle.
  • Every spend is charged to exactly one reservation: a build or fix seat to its ticket's, the auto PM seat to pm, and every review run, whatever provider ends up taking it, to that provider's reviews. The budget is an allowance enforced at admission, not an exact ledger. Overshoot is bounded by 0.5 × the open reservations, plus one review run per provider, plus whatever spend is in flight when a safe stop is sent.
  • At each window's start, before anything else, sm rolls over reservations. Every unreleased or suspended ticket reservation from the most recent non-skipped window gets a row in the new window of max(1, 1.5 × points − cumulative spend by the ticket): the full spend it may still make before its safe stop. The old row is released. sm then wakes each rolled-over ticket's claim holder with [sm autopm] budget restored: resume #N. In a skipped window there is no rollover; suspended rows wait for the next window that is not skipped. Rollovers are admitted first and may exceed the new allowance; anything left for later admissions is reduced accordingly. The 1.5× over-estimate check uses the ticket's cumulative spend against its original points.
  • Then, at each window's start, and on enable, sm creates two standing reservations per provider, admitted in this order and each capped by what is still unreserved: pm = (repo="autopm", number=0) of pm_points, then reviews = (repo="autopm", number=1) of review_points. Their spend never releases them early; they release at the window's end.
  • The auto PM seat's own spend, helpers included, counts against pm, and the same 1.5× safe stop applies to it. Before spawning a helper it checks pm.left from sm autopm budget, and spawns none at zero. If a window opens with a pm hold of 0 (rollovers took the allowance), sm sends the seat the safe-stop message at once. It stays idle until a window gives it a nonzero hold, and sm wakes it then.
  • Autopm reviews run one at a time per provider. A review is dispatched only while that provider's allowance for the current window is above zero. A second autopm review for a provider waits in the review queue until the first finishes, so at most one review per provider is ever unreserved spend in flight. When sm resolves the reviewer for an autopm PR (review/mod.rs chain()), it skips any provider whose reviews.left is ≤ 0, as it already skips a provider over its weekly meter. If no provider remains, the review waits for the next window, and sm tells the requesting agent [sm autopm] review budget spent until <reset>.

F. Measurement and feedback

F.1 Plan contents. Each experiment's plan is a short HTML doc in docs/autopm/plans/<id>.html, opened as a PR and reviewed with sm request-review (Codex per D.2). It must state:

  • the hypothesis;
  • the evidence from the activity log;
  • the change;
  • its path (switch or shadow) and why, and for shadow whether the post-approval build has a switch;
  • metric_json and guards_json per F.2, with the committed queries and the current baseline values;
  • the window;
  • side effects: "none", or each lasting effect listed (which decides the path);
  • a mock for every visible change, one per affected surface (Android, web, doc reader), drawn inline as HTML or SVG;
  • the points estimate;
  • the build tickets.

Converged means the review raised no P1, or the auto PM answered each P1 per CLAUDE.md's review loop.

F.2 Metric contract. sm runs every measurement, not the auto PM, so a successor agent cannot reinterpret one.

metric_json = {"query": "docs/autopm/metrics/<id>.sql",
               "stat": "median" | "mean" | "per_day",
               "direction": "lower" | "higher",
               "min_change_pct": 25}
guards_json = [{"query": "...sql", "stat": "...", "bad_direction": "higher" | "lower",
                "max_worse_pct": 25}, ...]
  • Query. A single SQLite SELECT, run read-only at plan_commit. The databases are attached as schemas q (queue database), act (activity.db), usage, util and bugs. The bound parameters are :from_ms and :until_ms. It returns rows (at_ms INTEGER, value REAL), one per observation, with from_ms ≤ at_ms < until_ms. Rows with a NULL value are ignored. The timeout is 30 s.
  • Stats. median is the median of values, averaging the two middle values when n is even. mean is the mean of values. per_day is the row count divided by the window length in days.
  • Windows. Baseline B = [baseline_until − 14 d, baseline_until). baseline_until_ms is set once, at the experiment's first go-live, and never changes, so a re-enabled experiment is still compared with the period before it first went live. Halves H1 = [live_at, live_at + window/2) and H2 = [live_at + window/2, live_at + window).
  • Bar. For each half, change = (stat(H) − stat(B)) / stat(B). It meets the bar when change ≤ −min_change_pct/100 for lower, or ≥ +min_change_pct/100 for higher. If stat(B) = 0, both the switch-on gate and the switchless go-live gate refuse with baseline_zero, and the experiment stays dark.
  • Guards. Each guard is computed the same way, and fails in a half when it moves in bad_direction by more than max_worse_pct. When a guard's baseline stat is 0, the percentage is undefined, and one rule replaces it for every stat. A per_day guard with bad_direction=higher fails when the half has 2 or more rows per day; with bad_direction=lower it cannot worsen and never fails. A median or mean guard fails when the half's stat is non-zero in bad_direction. Two guards are built in and cannot be removed: any 👎 vote, and the owner switching the switch off.
  • Decision, at measure_at_ms:
    • The rules apply in this order, and the first that matches wins:
    • (1) revert if any guard fails;
    • (2) if either half has fewer than 10 observations: extend once (extended=1, measure_at_ms += 14 × 86 400 000, halves recomputed over the longer window), or revert with note "insufficient data" if already extended;
    • (3) keep if both halves meet the bar;
    • (4) otherwise revert.
  • Errors. A query error or timeout records status=error and retries hourly. After 3 errors, the experiment is reverted with note "metric failed".
  • Revert sets the switch off with changed_by=autopm, not held. A switchless shadow experiment that misses its bar wakes the auto PM to file the manual rollback per B.6, and the readout states it. Each decision writes a row to autopm_measurements and wakes the auto PM.
  • Result shape: {"baseline": {stat, n}, "h1": {stat, n, change}, "h2": {…}, "guards": [{query, baseline, h1, h2, failed}]}.
  • Immediate revert: a 👎 at any time turns the switch off at once (held=1, changed_by=feedback), without waiting for the measurement date.

A kept change keeps its switch for 30 days. If you turn it off during that time, or it gets a 👎, the experiment becomes reverted, and the cleanup ticket removes the new code path and the switch. Otherwise, after 30 days with the switch still on and not held, the cleanup ticket removes the switch and the old code path. sm files both kinds of cleanup ticket itself, the day they fall due, via BoardSource::create_issue. Each goes under the experiment's cycle ticket, is recorded in autopm_fixes with kind=cleanup and the experiment id, and is auto-started with Tier: Mid under the C.4 reservation rules. After a keep-cleanup merges, the experiment's switch row is deleted and its state stays kept.

F.3 Feedback. A "What changed" card appears in Settings › Auto PM (Android and web), and as a dismissible chip on any screen a live switch changes. It lists live experiments: title, one line, live since, 👍 / 👎. POST /client/autopm/feedback {experiment_id, vote: "up"|"down", note?} (owner only) increments the counts. A down vote also switches the experiment off and wakes the auto PM.

F.4 The auto PM's own score, in every readout: experiments kept / finished, points spent per kept experiment, votes, changes you reverted or switched off yourself. A cycle counts toward the pause only if at least one experiment received a measurement decision (keep or revert) during it; cycles with nothing decided neither count nor reset the run. After four counting cycles in a row with no keep, sm sets autopm.paused_at, an owner-setting field separate from enabled. While paused, sm creates no cycles, admits no new experiments, and auto-starts nothing except cleanup tickets. Switches, including those of kept experiments, are left exactly as they are. When no experiment is live or measuring, sm tells the auto PM seat to publish a final readout and go idle, and sends one owner message. It never sets enabled=false; that stays the kill switch (B.5). Clearing paused_at in Settings ("Resume") resumes normal cycles. The owner message reads: "Auto PM paused: four cycles, nothing kept. Readouts: …".

Z. FAQ

Why one standing seat instead of a fresh agent per cycle? Rajesh's call. The playbook and the handoff note carry continuity; one seat lets reminders and switches have a single owner.

Why not let research run on a cheap tier and judgment on the top tier, as separate seats? Rajesh rejected the split. The auto PM is one judgment seat, and research helpers live in its context.

Why are restart and deploy scripts ask-only when a commit revert would undo them? Undoing a change goes through those scripts. A broken one can block its own revert.

Why does Analytics › Time not suffice? It attributes agent time, including time spent waiting on "you". It records nothing about what Rajesh does on each surface, which is the auto PM's main input.

Can it tell the Claude app from the terminal? No. The prompt hook carries no origin. Both are agent_prompt.

What about Codex accounts other than the build account? Allowance applies to the account the build agents resolve to. Others are untouched.
