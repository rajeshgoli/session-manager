2479 — Near-idle queue jobs give back their slot

Author: Claude Opus 5.5 92ff4310 · Ticket #2479 · 21 Oct 2026

Memo

Recommendation: a running background or service job that has used almost no CPU, no GPU and little memory for 30 minutes stops counting against the queue's job limits, so waiting work can start. The moment it gets busy again it counts again. sm never stops or slows it. Over the last 23 days this would have unblocked a waiting job in 26 of the 56 hours that some job sat waiting for a slot, and only 3 of 95 jobs that went near-idle later got busy again.

One fix has to land first. The queue is stuck right now for a different reason, filed as #2480: once start-now put a fourth service job on a service limit of 2, every admission pass refuses to start any job. Until that is fixed, freeing slots does nothing. #2479 is recorded on the board as starting after #2480.

Slot
One unit of the queue's job limits. Two limits apply to every job: the global limit (max_running_jobs, 8 on this Mac) and its type's limit (background 2, service 2, tests 6, perf 1). A running job takes one slot from each. A pending job starts only when both have a free slot.
Near-idle job
A running background or service job that, over the last 30 minutes, averaged under 5% of one CPU core, used no GPU time, and never held more than 2 GiB of memory. It holds no slot. Log output does not matter: a watcher that prints a status line every minute is still near-idle.
Wake
A near-idle job wakes when, over any 5 minutes, it averages above 25% of one core, uses any GPU time, or holds more than 4 GiB. It takes its slots back at once, even if that puts the queue over its limits.
Quiet (existing, unchanged)
The existing "Quiet since …" alert: no CPU, GPU or log output for 10 minutes. It warns that a job may be hung. Near-idle is a separate, capacity-only judgment; a job can be near-idle and not quiet.

Appendices

A. The rule, exactly

Inputs. The utilization recorder already writes one job_samples row per running job every 5 seconds: cumulative CPU seconds, cumulative GPU seconds, footprint bytes (RSS when footprint is missing)1. Near-idle is computed from those rows only. Log bytes are not an input.

Config (new block under queue_runner; all optional, defaults shown):

queue_runner:
  near_idle:
    enabled: true
    minutes: 30            # idle window
    cpu_percent: 5         # % of ONE core, averaged over the window
    memory_gib: 2          # max footprint at any sample in the window
    wake_minutes: 5
    wake_cpu_percent: 25   # % of one core, averaged over any wake window
    wake_memory_gib: 4     # footprint at any single sample

enabled: false restores today's behaviour exactly. Validation: cpu_percent < wake_cpu_percent, memory_gib < wake_memory_gib, minutes ≥ wake_minutes ≥ 1; otherwise config load fails with a message naming the key.

Entering near-idle. A running job of type background or service becomes near-idle at the first sample t where all hold:

  1. t − started_at ≥ minutes, and the recorder has samples of this job covering [t − minutes, t] with no gap over 60 s (the same coverage rule the existing low-CPU check uses2). A gap restarts the window.
  2. CPU growth over the window, summing only positive deltas between consecutive samples (a process exit drops the cumulative counter), is < cpu_percent/100 × window seconds.
  3. GPU cumulative seconds did not increase between any two consecutive samples in the window.
  4. Every sample's footprint in the window is ≤ memory_gib. A missing reading (both footprint and RSS null) counts as a failure, not a pass.

Waking. A near-idle job stops being near-idle at the first sample t where any holds: positive CPU growth over [t − wake_minutes, t] > wake_cpu_percent/100 × that span; GPU seconds increased since the previous sample; footprint at t > wake_memory_gib. After waking it must satisfy the entry rule again from scratch (a fresh full window starting after the wake).

Worked example. A watcher starts at 10:00 and spends 2 minutes at 1.5 cores and 600 MiB setting up, then polls a cloud API at 0.2% of a core and 150 MiB. At 10:32 the 30-minute window [10:02, 10:32] holds only polling: CPU 0.2% < 5%, no GPU, 150 MiB ≤ 2 GiB. It becomes near-idle at 10:32 and an admission pass runs. At 13:10 it downloads results and its footprint reaches 5 GiB: it wakes at that sample, counts again, and if the queue now holds 9 slot-takers against a limit of 8, nothing new starts until one finishes or something else goes near-idle. Earliest it can be near-idle again: 13:40.

Not eligible, ever: tests, perf, review, shell-adopted jobs (shell jobs already take no slot3), and pending jobs.

Restart. The near-idle flag is held in memory by the recorder and is not persisted. On a server start every running job holds a slot. Because the window reads stored job_samples rows, a job whose last 30 minutes of samples straddle the restart can be near-idle at the first recorder tick, provided the coverage rule holds (a restart gap over 60 s breaks coverage, so in practice the window restarts). Either outcome is correct; no test pins which.

B. Admission changes

One predicate. Define "holds a slot" as: state == running, not review, not shell, and not near-idle. Every place that counts running jobs against a limit uses it:

Where the flag lives. The recorder publishes a map job_id → near_idle_since_ms alongside the existing quiet STATUS map10, keyed by queue DB path the same way. Admission reads it while holding the admission lock. A job missing from the map holds a slot. If the recorder is disabled (no utilization config) the map stays empty and behaviour equals today.

Triggering admission. When any job turns near-idle in a recorder tick, the recorder requests an admission pass for that state dir (through the same path the existing admission retry uses11). A wake needs no pass: it only makes admission stricter.

Hold reason. Unchanged: a job blocked by a limit still holds concurrency_cap.

E. How the evidence was computed

Read-only copy of ~/.local/share/claude-sessions/utilization.db, snapshot 21 Oct 20:37 local, 553 hours of samples. For every running background/service job, slide a 30-minute window over its samples and apply rules A.2–A.4 with 5% and 2 GiB; "woke" in the memo table means a later 5-minute window above 1 core or a footprint at or above 4 GiB (a stricter bar than the wake rule, chosen to count only material wake-ups). "Hours some job waited" sums sample intervals (capped at 10 s) where any pending job had holding_reason = concurrency_cap; that reason covers per-type limits as well as the global one, so 26 of 56 is an upper bound on hours actually unblocked. The three wake-ups: 2066-nq5-money (19 GiB), 1858-upload-r2 (9 GiB), backup-first-run (1.3 cores).

Z. FAQ

Why not just raise the global limit to 16? It treats a busy 48 GiB job and a sleeping watcher alike; two more busy jobs would oversubscribe the Mac with no signal. The limit means "real work running at once"; near-idle keeps it meaning that.

Why does memory count at all if CPU is the contended resource? A job holding 9 GiB while idle (2171-watch8 today) is often between phases of real work. Counting it is conservative; the 2 GiB bar still frees every pure watcher seen today.

Should near-idle jobs be exempt from perf displacement? Out of scope. Displacement is about giving perf a quiet machine and a watcher can be resubmitted; changing it is a separate call.

Why not persist the flag across restarts? It is derived entirely from stored samples, so there is nothing to persist that the samples do not already hold; recomputing from them is simpler and cannot go stale.
