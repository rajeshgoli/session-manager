2543 — One app: the terminal, documents and pages share one frame

Author: Claude (Opus 5.5) 0ba7dfd9 · Ticket #2543 · 2026-10-27 · Revision 9

Memo

I recommend that the terminal page's layout become the layout of the whole web app. Every page gets one left sidebar. It holds the page links as today, the live agents and shells (the list that today exists only on the terminal page), compact usage meters, and Settings. On a terminal the sidebar puts agents first; on every other page it puts pages first. The Agents page goes away: resting the pointer on an agent in the sidebar shows its card, and sm.rajeshgo.li/ opens a terminal. Documents open full width instead of in the side panel, under a bar that matches the terminal's. The top bar shows only what you are looking at, with the moon and the bug button at its right end on every screen.

From a terminal, any page is one click away. From any page, any agent is one click away. ⌘[ returns to the terminal you just left, still connected.

Sidebar
The single left column on every page, 248 points wide: the terminal session list's width, not the 144 points of today's left column. Page links keep today's left-column style; agent rows keep the session list's. From the top: "sm" and the search box (⌘K); the page links; the Agents heading and the live agents and shells; the usage meters; Settings. It folds to a thin strip with ⌘\ or a click on that strip.
Pages first, agents first
The sidebar's two arrangements. Pages first: every page link as today, and each agent on a one-line row. Agents first: page links as one row of icons, and each agent on today's two-line row. Pages first is the default on pages; agents first is the default on a terminal. The chevron after "10 live" swaps them.
Hover card
The card the Agents page shows today for one agent (task, provider, ticket, repo, state, jobs, latest fact, context ring), shown when the pointer rests on that agent's row in the sidebar.
Focus view
A page about one thing that wants the whole width: an agent's or shell's terminal, or a document.
Top bar
The bar above the main area. Left: what you are looking at. Right: actions on it. Every bar ends the same way: the moon, the bug button, then the view's main action in the top-right corner, where today's Agents page has New agent. The main action is Retire on a terminal, Review on a document, New note on Notes, and New agent everywhere else.
Side panel
The resizable column on the right: an agent's details and message thread, a queue job, a ticket, Notes.

The screens

All five screens are drawn at the size of the window in your screenshots, 1512 × 820, with ten live agents. They use the app's own colours, fonts and icons.

What moves and what stays

  • Stays: every page link (Inbox, Board, Queue, Artifacts, Notes, Analytics, History, Guestbook), Settings at the bottom on its own, and the moon and bug button at the top right. The moon and bug button now also appear on the terminal and document bars.
  • Moves into the sidebar: search goes to the top. New agent keeps its top-right corner on pages, and the Agents heading gains a + for it, so you can start an agent from a terminal too. Queue status ("1 · 0") sits beside the Queue link.
  • Moves into the top bar: on a terminal, the agent's ticket number, title and repo fill today's empty space (Screen 1). The Notes page's "+ New" becomes its top-right action, "New note".
  • Goes away: the Agents page. Its cards become hover cards (Screen 2); the rest is placed below.
  • Becomes smaller: the meters, from about 300 points to about 75 (Screen 5).
  • Moves out of the side panel: documents, which open full width (Screen 3).

Why this design

The terminal page already had the right layout. Its left column lists the agents, which is what you navigate by. Today's left column lists pages, and today's Agents page is a large version of the agents list. One column holding both gives one place to go anywhere. Slack and Linear use this layout for the same reason.

It fits, measured on your screenshots. A page link is 32 points, an agent's two-line row 48, its one-line row 26, a section heading 26.

  • Pages first takes about 730 of 820 points: search 40, eight page links 256, heading 34, ten one-line rows and group gaps 290, meters 75, Settings 36. The one-line rows drop the section headings; each dot's colour shows the section.
  • Agents first takes about 810: search 40, icon row 36, heading 34, ten two-line rows with headings 590, meters 75, Settings 36. Shells and anything past ten agents scroll.

The meters show only what you need to know. The server already computes each quota's pace: when it will run out, or what share it will reach at reset. A line under a provider appears only when a limit will run out before reset, or when most of the week will go unused. The Mac shows only readings that are already amber or red on today's scale; otherwise it says "quiet". sm does not measure disk today, so disk is not shown.

Focus views stay immersive and look alike. The terminal keeps today's width and bar. A document gets the full width, because the side panel is a poor place to read. Its bar follows the terminal's pattern (Screen 3). Left: what it is (title, "Revision 5" rather than a commit hash, the pull request). A magenta note, "2 replies on changed passages · Read", sits in the bar while the author has answered comments on passages it has since changed, and leaves once you read them. Today that note is a banner inside the document that is gone once closed. Right: who wrote it (the author agent, one click to its terminal; Open in Claude), Print, a ⋯ menu for Print all and new tab, then the moon and bug button, and Review in the top-right corner, where New agent sits on other pages. Ask goes: you have not used it, and the Inbox thread with the author does the same job.

Cost. On Board and Queue the sidebar is wider than today's left column (15.5 rem against 9 rem, about 100 points), because it now holds agents. ⌘\ folds it on any page.

Submitting a review answers at once

Today "Submit review" holds the sheet for 2–3 seconds. In that time sm looks up the pull request on GitHub, saves your review, posts it and its comments to GitHub, and wakes the agent. Only then does the sheet say "Review posted", with a Close button you nearly always press.

Instead, sm answers as soon as it has saved your review, in well under a second. The sheet closes by itself, and the document bar says "Review sent". GitHub posting and the agent's wake happen afterwards, without you. The bar shows progress only when something is wrong:

What happenedWhat the document bar shows
Saved, posting to GitHub"Review sent" for a few seconds; nothing else to do
PostedNothing more. The agent gets its usual "[sm review]" wake with the GitHub link.
GitHub down or slowNothing for the first minute. After that, a muted "Posting review…" while sm keeps retrying for up to an hour.
GitHub refused it, or an hour of retries failedA red note, "Review not posted: reason · Submit again". It stays until the review posts or you discard it.

This is safe because sm already writes a review down before it touches GitHub, and a resubmission of the same review can never post twice. Checks that can turn a review away still answer before the sheet closes, for example another device's unfinished review of the same revision. A server restart picks up any review it had not finished posting.

E. Terminal view and kept connection

  1. Inside the terminal page, the old .term-side column (switcher and edge strip) is removed; the sidebar replaces it. Retire still moves to the next row by today's rowAfterRetire, computed on the sidebar's order.
  2. One kept terminal. App() holds kept = { id, leftAt }. Leaving a terminal view for any non-terminal view sets it. The TerminalPage for kept.id stays mounted with the same key inside a wrapper with the hidden attribute. Returning to /terminal/kept.id un-hides it. Opening a different terminal unmounts the kept one first with today's teardown.
  3. No resize while hidden. A hidden element measures 0×0; a resize from it would shrink the agent's real tmux window. While hidden the page runs no fit and sends no resize frame. On un-hide it fits once and sends one resize only if the size differs from the last one sent.
  4. Focus. On hide, the terminal is blurred, so keys typed elsewhere never reach the agent. On un-hide it takes focus.
  5. While hidden, output is written to the off-screen terminal, pings continue, reconnects proceed as today.
  6. After 15 minutes hidden the app unmounts it and clears kept; a timer, cleared on return. If the session stops or the connection reaches Ended while hidden, the same happens immediately.
  7. pagehide/pageshow handling is unchanged and covers the kept terminal.
  8. Tab title on a terminal view: <session name> · sm.

  3. Back. The ← button and ⌘[ do history.back() when the previous entry is in this app, else navigate to /. Because opening a document is a pushState, Back from a document reached from a terminal lands on that terminal, which is still connected (E.2).
  4. Every opener switches from openPanel('doc:…') to navigate('/read' + path): web/inbox.js:121, 229, 267, 268, 277; web/history.js:110; web/reader.js:100, 149 (links inside a document stay in the document view); web/ui.js:485; terminal file and web links whose target is a same-origin reader path (web/terminal.js openLink).   6. Unchanged: a direct load of sm.rajeshgo.li/docs/… (published links, the phone app) still serves the standalone document page. Its existing "full screen" path (fullScreen) is removed from the app, because the document view is already full width.
  7. Tab title: <document title> · sm.
  8. Ask removed. The Reader's Ask button, its doc-ask panel and its keyboard shortcut go from the web app. Server routes are untouched; the Android app's thread screen keeps its own use.

In the document view the bar shows "n repl(y|ies) on changed passages · Read" in magenta while the count is above zero; Read calls showChangedThreads(), which marks them seen as today, and the note leaves. The in-document banner line for this notice is not shown when the page is inside the app (other banner lines stay); a direct load of /docs/… keeps today's banner.

J. Instant review submit

  1. What stays synchronous in submit_owner_doc_review (http/docs/review.rs): request validation (submission id, full sha, verdict, body); the review lock; an existing row with this id (posted: return it, 200; queued or submitting: return 202 with its status and do nothing else; failed: requeue it, J.3); and the superseded check (another id's unfinished review of this revision: 409 as today). No GitHub or git call happens before the response.
  2. New first state. The review row gains status queued: the row is written with doc, sha, verdict, body, hold flag and submission id, and with target and blob sha empty. The response is 202 {"submission_id", "status": "queued"}. unfinished_review counts queued as unfinished, so the superseded rule keeps holding.
  3. Background worker. A server task takes queued rows one at a time, under the same review lock. For each one it runs today's steps unchanged: the pull request lookup, reopen::target when there is no open PR (branch name and hold as today), the merge hold for an open PR, loading the doc bytes, filling target and blob sha (moving the row to submitting), and then run_blocking, which posts, sets posted and wakes the agent as today. The worker wakes on each new row and at startup, so rows left queued or submitting by a restart finish. A submitting row reconciles against GitHub by its marker first, as a client retry does today.
  4. Retries. Transient failures (network, GitHub 5xx or rate limit, a failed spawn) retry after 5 s, 30 s, 2 min, 10 min, then every 15 min, until one hour after the row was written. Then the row becomes failed with the last error. A refusal (GitHub 4xx, a reopen target that refuses, comments on files the PR does not change) becomes failed at once with its message. "Submit again" in the bar re-posts the same submission id, which requeues a failed row with the same verdict and body. "Discard" deletes the failed row, and its drafts stay as drafts.
  5. Head. GET …/head adds review_submission: the newest owner submission for this doc that is not posted, or posted within the last 15 s: {submission_id, status, error, queued_at, github_review_url}, or null.
  6. Client (http/doc_client.js). On 202 or 200: clear drafts for this sha as today, close the sheet, and show "Review sent" in the bar for 4 s (inside the app this is the document bar; on a direct /docs/… load it is today's in-document bar). The page then polls head every 3 s while review_submission is queued or submitting. Past 60 s in those states it shows the muted "Posting review…". On failed it shows the red note with Submit again and Discard; on posted it shows nothing. The existing 409 paths (other device, changed drafts) still render in the sheet before it closes. The "Review posted / Open it on GitHub / Close" screen goes.
  7. Agent side unchanged. The agent's wake still fires only after GitHub has the review, so the link in it always resolves.
  8. Example: you submit with GitHub answering in 2.4 s. The response returns in about 50 ms, and the sheet closes. 2.5 s later the row is posted and the agent is woken. The bar has shown "Review sent" and then nothing.

Z. FAQ

How did the design get here? Revision 1 kept the page links as a narrow icon column beside the terminal's agents list; the owner found two left columns overloaded and bolted on, and it left the Agents page and documents unaddressed. Revision 2 merged both lists into one sidebar, made the Agents page the overview behind the heading, and moved documents out of the side panel; it did not fit at real sizes. Revision 3 makes the sidebar's parts take turns, sized on real screenshots for 8–10 agents. Revision 4 gives the top bar one rule (only about what you are looking at). Revision 5 keeps every page link, Settings and the moon where the owner has them, takes the room from compact, importance-based meters instead of a More menu, and shows the design as screens. Revision 6 removes the Agents page on the evidence of 14 days of use, puts the ticket on the terminal bar, and redesigns the document bar. Revision 7 fixes the moon and bug button as the last two items on every bar, keeps Print, removes Ask, moves the replies notice into the bar, hides the five-hour meter until it matters, and states the sidebar width. Revision 8 makes Submit review answer once sm has saved the review. Revision 9 puts each view's main action in the top-right corner with the moon and bug button to its left. The separate "Terminal" page link and g t shortcut of revision 1 are gone: every agent is in the sidebar.

Why 15 minutes for the kept terminal? It covers a look at Board, Queue or a document and back, which is what the second tab is used for, without a forgotten terminal streaming over the Cloudflare relay all afternoon. It is a constant.

Why only one kept terminal? Moving between agents through the sidebar reconnects today and is not part of the complaint. Keeping several multiplies streams for little gain.

Why not put page links in a top bar? It frees width, but the terminal and document views need their bar for their own title and actions, so pages would need a second bar, costing two terminal rows on every screen.

Does the Android app change? No. It has native navigation and its own terminal and document screens.
