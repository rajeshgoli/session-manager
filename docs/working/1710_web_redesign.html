1710 — sm on the web: one page for everything

Author: Claude (design) sm-web-redesign-spec-author · Ticket #1710 · 29 September 2026 · every screen below is drawn from live sm data at 5:25 pm PT that day

Memo

sm.rajeshgo.li becomes one page on the laptop that does everything the phone app and the Studio do. A left-hand menu replaces the five tabs. A side panel opens any agent, doc, queue job or ticket without leaving the page. The page follows the Mac's light or dark setting. It keeps the phone app's look (its palette, context rings and cards) and adds what only a wide screen can show: timelines, two-pane reading, and a live terminal.

One idea runs through every screen: colour tells you who has the ball. The server already knows this. It is scattered across the agent state, queue jobs, Codex reviews and your inbox, and today you piece it together in your head. The redesign shows it as a single status per ticket and per agent:

Decisions

QuestionRecommendationWhy
1. Adopt this direction: one page, left menu, side panel, colour meaning who has the ball?Yes. After you approve it, I write the build spec as appendices here and split it into tickets.It answers each complaint in your list with one design rather than seven patches.
2. Terminal in the browser?Yes, full typing. It is protected by the same Cloudflare login as the rest of the site, and only your Google account passes that login.It is the one thing you can do at the Studio but not from the web today.
3. Where do settings live?On the server, shared with the phone. They replace the board.start_defaults config entry. Theme stays per browser.You set a default once, and it applies on every device.
4. When is a ticket “stalled” (red)?After 15 minutes in progress with nothing moving: the agent is not working, no job is queued or running, no review is pending, and nothing waits on you.Short enough to catch a stuck critical path, long enough to ignore a pause between turns.
5. Should Settings let you change queue slot limits?Yes, per job type, taking effect without a restart. The Queue page links there only when a limit is holding work back.Today the limit is a config edit and a restart. The 24-hour numbers say it cost real time.
6. How is it built?A single web app that sm-server serves, with no separate build step. The phone keeps its current pages until the app moves over.cargo build and the restart script stay the only way to ship, and the app is not broken in the meantime.

Appendices

A. Palette and type

Dark keeps the phone app's palette (android-app/…/ui/theme/Color.kt), which today's web already mirrors (owner_docs.rs, page_shell). Light is new, with each status colour darkened until it reads on white. Every colour is a token with a light value and a dark value. The page follows prefers-color-scheme unless Settings › Appearance overrides it.

Type uses the Mac system face for the interface and SF Mono for ticket numbers, job labels and the terminal. The type scale is 11, 12.5, 13.5, 17 and 22 px. Numbers that line up in columns use tabular figures. Chips are filled with their status colour at 14% strength and have no border. A selected item gets a filled background, never a border or a change of weight, so nothing shifts when you select it. The context ring's colour is the phone's continuous blend from green at 0% through amber at 50% to red at 100% (HandoffControls.kt, ContextHandoffStatus).

Z. FAQ

Why not embed claude.ai? claude.ai sends X-Frame-Options: SAMEORIGIN, so browsers refuse to show it inside any other site. I checked this on 29 September 2026. Opening it in a reused tab is the closest you can get.

Does the phone app change? Not in this work. The phone keeps loading today's pages. A later pass can give the app the light theme and the ticket clock, since both come from the same server data.

Why are the Iter8 strips only three hours long? The window can be 3, 6 or 24 hours. Three hours fits today's example. In these mockups, the segment before each agent's first queued job is drawn from the agent's last recorded activity. The shipped version reads Analytics › Time for the exact split.
